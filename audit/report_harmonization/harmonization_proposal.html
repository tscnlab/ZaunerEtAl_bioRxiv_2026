Decision summary

The corpus can be made substantially easier to read without changing its science. The accepted figures are already polished, and the numerical displays are generally publication-formatted. The main work is to replace internal production history with current scientific meaning, explain recurring specialist terms consistently, make every main finding visible early, normalize report structure, use dynamic QMD links, and provide one complete anchored deviation reference.

The author decided the five bounded items as follows:

  1. shared vocabulary and first-use explanations;
  2. structural templates and the listed scientific exceptions;
  3. cross-link and navigation plan;
  4. main/supplement figure and table shortlist, small styling adjustments, and the native-gt table contract—authorized for a first adjustment run, with final visual approval retained;
  5. preregistration-deviation document structure and inclusion tiers.

The complete read-only audit is in the Phase 1 corpus audit. The corpus inventory records all 38 current QMDs, owner tasks, scientific status, source hashes, navigation positions, and expected renders. The unfinished H06_daily analysis has no accepted reader-facing Stage 3/4 QMD and is explicitly deferred.

Non-negotiable boundary

Every authorized change is display-only. Owners must not change data, models, estimates, intervals, p-values, diagnostics, sensitivity results, multiplicity families, sample definitions, or scientific claims. They must not refit, predict, simulate, bootstrap, rerun Shapley analyses, rebuild shared preparation, or trigger a full-project render.

If editing reveals a possible scientific discrepancy, that document stops. The owner returns a scoped issue to the responsible scientific task or coordinator. The harmonization task does not resolve such discrepancies editorially.

Current scoped holds and qualifications are:

  • index.qmd remains on hold until the coordinator reconciles its legacy RQ claims and assets with accepted H01–H11 results.
  • Preparation 03 must retain the plain qualification that independent reconstruction of the current reference-profile input remains incomplete.
  • Preparation 04 and H10 must retain the plain qualification that independent reconstruction of current diary-period support remains incomplete.
  • H02 needs a display-description correction: its rendered main figure shows sites in panel B and participant curves in panel C, while current prose and alt text reverse those panels. Stored curves and results remain untouched.
  • H05 must retain the finding that none of 68 primary associations retained adjusted support and that four sleep-environment models are unfit for inference.
  • H07 must not turn its derivative-defined rule into a biological ceiling or unconditional plateau claim.
  • H11 must retain separate placement curves and the distinction between a global curve test and pointwise descriptive intervals.
  • The accepted hourly H06 report is the main H06 result. H06_daily is complementary and enters later without reopening the hourly report unnecessarily.

Approved shared vocabulary

The following terms would become the reader-facing defaults. Technical terms remain where they identify a model, equation, diagnostic, or source-data field precisely, but only after a concise first-use explanation.

[predictor] heterogeneity [predictor]-by-site interaction The association was allowed to differ by study site.
equal-site estimate site-average estimate An average across sites that gives each site equal weight.
GAM or GAMM without context nonlinear GAM analysis or nonlinear GAM model Explain that a generalized additive model (GAM) lets the named association bend rather than follow a straight line; GAM may remain afterward.
diagnostics model checks At first use, “model checks (sometimes called model diagnostics)” is acceptable.

autocorrelation or AR(1) autocorrelation, then AR(1) Explain why adjacent observations may remain more similar and that AR(1) represents decreasing correlation at larger time separations; AR(1) may remain afterward.

derivative derivative Explain once that it is the slope of the fitted curve and describes how quickly the fitted outcome changes; retain the term afterward.

Internal and historical terms

Reader reports would no longer lead with stage, gate, worker, task, coordinator, frozen, sealed, pinned, production artifact, manifest, checksum, SHA-256, V0, legacy implementation, submitted implementation, or internal METRIC-, FIND-, REPORT-, CHG-, and ENV- identifiers.

Where an unresolved scientific qualification exists, it is stated directly. For example:

Independent reconstruction of the current diary-period support remains incomplete.

Technical identifiers, hashes, and implementation histories remain available in audit records or concise reproducibility sections. Stable deviation IDs are different: they remain visible because they provide durable reader links.

Approved structural templates

These are shared information orders, not rigid word counts. A section can be absent when it has no content, but the reason for a substantive departure is recorded.

Result report

  1. Question and scope: one short paragraph stating the scientific question, placement role, and outcome family.
  2. Answer in brief: an exact note callout—::: {.callout-note title="Answer in brief"}—with the main result, inferential scope, and most important limitation in two to four sentences.
  3. What was analysed: exact participant, participant-day/observation, and site support; primary versus complementary placement; exact linked deviations.
  4. Model and reported quantities: plain model description, reference conditions, scale, confidence intervals, and multiplicity family; technical model name second.
  5. Primary near-eye result: main figure and main table early, followed by only the detail needed to interpret them.
  6. Complementary chest result: same vocabulary, clearly described as complementary rather than automatically replicated.
  7. Same-participant/day placement comparison: only when scientifically valid; say what is matched and what the comparison cannot establish.
  8. Model checks: concise reader assessment, with detailed diagnostics in the companion or supplement.
  9. Sensitivity analyses: organized by what changed, with interpretation stability stated directly.
  10. Interpretation and limitations: strongest defensible result, uncertainty, practical meaning, and boundaries.
  11. Analysis record and source data: reciprocal QMD link to the companion and restrained links to stored source data.

Preparation/provenance companion

  1. About this analysis record: purpose, scientific target, reciprocal dynamic link to the result.
  2. Analysis map: short route from accepted inputs to reported outputs.
  3. Render boundary: state that the report displays stored outputs and does not refit or recompute.
  4. Inputs and preparation: authoritative data, versions where scientifically consequential, exclusions, and deviation links.
  5. Sample and support: exact units, denominators, placement role, and support limitations.
  6. Model and estimands: formula and technical details after a plain explanation.
  7. Model checks and influence: scientific assessment first, detailed values second.
  8. Sensitivity analyses: scenario, matching unit, and effect on interpretation.
  9. Outputs and reproducibility: source tables/figures, environment, formulas, and hashes in a restrained technical section.
  10. Reader takeaway: optional one-paragraph bridge back to the result when the companion is unusually technical.

Descriptives

  1. Study sample and sites.
  2. Data coverage and placement roles.
  3. Main descriptive figure and table.
  4. Light distributions and temporal context.
  5. Important data-quality or denominator qualifications.
  6. Dynamic links to preparation details and source data.

Replication history, export pilots, hashes, and manifest comparisons move out of the main reader flow.

Preparation pages

  1. Why this preparation step matters.
  2. Inputs and relevant definitions.
  3. Processing decisions in scientific order.
  4. Resulting data and exact sample/support consequences.
  5. Limitations or unresolved qualifications.
  6. Material links to adjacent preparation pages and exact deviations.
  7. Technical provenance, hashes, and verifier evidence at the end or in an audit link.

Glossary boxes are approved only when a document introduces at least three specialized terms that recur and cannot be explained smoothly at first use. They should contain three to six one-sentence definitions, appear before the first dense figure/table, and never become a generic vocabulary dump.

Proposed reader structure

  1. What this document contains: definition of a preregistration deviation and distinction from audit-only implementation/documentation records.
  2. How to read an entry: stable ID, topic, plain description, rationale, affected hypotheses, current consequence, and authoritative source link.
  3. Current scientific deviations: grouped by reader topic rather than workflow order:
    • data eligibility and sample definition;
    • time, state, placement, and reference definitions;
    • metric definitions and support;
    • model, estimand, and inferential specification;
    • sensitivity and reporting decisions.
  4. Superseded or audit-only records: a compact appendix retaining stable anchors and enough context to state why the item does not control the current analysis.
  5. Source and update note: central evidence paths, generation/check date, and scope.

Every represented stable record receives an anchor. Scientifically current or consequential entries receive the full reader entry. Documentation-only, environment, or superseded-history records remain in the audit-only appendix with the same required fields in concise form, so no ID disappears silently.

Entry pattern

### DEV-015: Plain topic title {#dev-015}

**Topic.** Data eligibility and sample definition.

**What differed.** Plain description of the preregistered or expected approach and the accepted approach.

**Why.** Reader-facing rationale grounded in the authoritative evidence.

**Affected hypotheses.** H01, H05, and H10.

**Consequence.** What changes for the sample, model, quantity, or interpretation; state explicitly when current results are unaffected.

The visible stable ID remains uppercase; the anchor is its lowercase form. No replacement ID is invented. A result link would look like:

[deviation DEV-015](../preregistration_deviations.qmd#dev-015)

Companions and preparation pages use the relative path appropriate to their source location.

Proposed inclusion tiers requiring approval

  • Full reader entry: actual preregistration/data/sample/metric/model/estimand/reporting departure or a consequential implementation repair that affects what was analysed or how it is interpreted.
  • Concise audit-only entry: environment migration, documentation gap, nonconsequential implementation record, or superseded history with no current interpretive consequence.

Both tiers retain stable anchors, topic, description, rationale, affected hypotheses, and consequence. The tier affects prominence and detail, not traceability.

Phase 3 structural tests

After the document exists, tests will fail on:

  • hard-coded internal .html, file://, absolute local, _build, or build-directory links;
  • unresolved relative QMD targets or anchors;
  • duplicate deviation IDs or anchors;
  • a register ID missing from the reader or audit-only index;
  • an unlinked reader-facing deviation mention;
  • a visible deviation ID whose link does not target its exact anchor.
