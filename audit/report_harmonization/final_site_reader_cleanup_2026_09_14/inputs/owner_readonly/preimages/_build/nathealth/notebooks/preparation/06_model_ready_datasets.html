Preparation 06: Build model-ready datasets

From checked metrics and participant information to hypothesis-specific inputs

Last modified:

August 13, 2026

Purpose and position in the analysis chain

Preparation 06 turns the checked light-exposure metrics from Preparation 04 and the fixed participant, questionnaire, and diary files acquired in Preparation 05 into inputs that hypothesis analyses can use. It first makes participant files structurally consistent, then adds participant and site context to the metric files, preserves the distinction between local clock time and actual elapsed time, and finally stores a separate input when a hypothesis method has been approved. H01 is the first such build. This step does not fit a statistical model.

The predefined gap-timing-unaware dataset still passed the general 50%-per-hour and 80%-per-day coverage rules. Its name means that the timing of remaining missing observations was not used for an additional metric-specific adjustment. For contrast, the primary dataset could be interpreted as a “time-sensitive primary metric dataset.” From this point onward, this report calls it simply the primary dataset.

The exact stored inputs are the current light-metric files, the fixed questionnaire and diary files, site and daylight context, elapsed-time records, and the separately prepared gap-timing-unaware dataset. Their file identities, producing scripts, and verification records are collected in the technical provenance section after the scientific sample consequences.

1. Standardize participant, questionnaire, and diary structures

The first operation reads only the fixed local files acquired in Preparation 05 and places site identifiers, participant identifiers, labels, column types, and time representations into a consistent structure. Elapsed time is represented in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC); time-of-day fields retain the site’s local clock time. This structural work is kept separate from later joins so that recorded values, missing values, and excluded free text can be checked before metrics and participant information meet.

The procedure did not recalculate questionnaire totals or factor scores. The 963 field-level value-preservation records all passed, and all 63 audited free-text fields were excluded from analytical RDS files without exporting their content.

Records without a complete interval remain in their prepared input file, but they cannot contribute to a calculation that requires both interval endpoints. The stored audit identifies 27 such light-exposure-diary records (one at Borås (SE) and 26 at Delft (NL)) and one Tübingen (DE) sleep-diary record without a wake time.

2. Keep the prepared-data comparison separate

The gap-timing-unaware dataset is assembled independently of the primary dataset. That separation prevents a preparation difference from being erased when both are later passed through the same hypothesis implementation.

The stored data cover participant-level, participant-day, 30-minute, and one-hour values. Exact metric-support hours cannot be recovered from these retained prepared artifacts; later tables therefore show those hours as unavailable rather than zero or a reconstructed estimate.

One objectively verified participant-identifier error is documented rather than silently changed: record DEV-056 assigns seven TUM exercise-diary rows to TUM_S001 instead of the recorded TUM_S101. The correction did not change a scientific light value.

3. Add site and daylight context

The site-context operation reads the approved site coordinates and time zones and the local dates required by both prepared datasets. It stores civil dawn, civil dusk, photoperiod, and descriptive solar-noon information for 618 site-date combinations across nine sites, including four site-dates with a daylight-saving transition.

The gap-timing-unaware input supplies supplemental site dates. The stored comparison found all 618 rows and all 47 context fields exactly unchanged across the documented input transition.

Every merge uses site and local date as a many-to-one key: many measurement rows may use one site-date context record. All six joins retained their input row counts, and none lacked a matching site-date record.

4. Preserve time of day and actual elapsed time

Local clock time and elapsed time answer different questions. A 30-minute row labelled 08:00 represents 08:00 at every study site, which is useful for time-of-day comparisons. UTC records how much real time separated two rows, which is necessary for continuity and autocorrelation. A separate temporal-sequence operation preserves both coordinates without changing melEDI values.

When clocks move back, a local-clock interval may represent two real intervals; its stored local-clock outcome is their average. When clocks move forward, a local-clock interval can have no corresponding real interval. Only an unambiguous, usable real interval can continue an elapsed-time sequence. A real gap, unusable outcome, repeated autumn interval, or structural spring gap starts or interrupts a sequence.

5. Combine checked data without choosing a hypothesis sample

The combination step reads the checked metric files, standardized participant inputs, and site context. It adds one matching participant record and one matching site/date record to each measurement row. It does not apply a complete-case filter or a hypothesis-specific exclusion. Instead, it records which questionnaire and diary inputs are available, allowing each hypothesis to state its own requirements later.

The shared primary near-eye input contains 141 participants, 816 participant-days, and 39,168 30-minute grid rows. The complementary chest input contains 154 participants, 902 participant-days, and 43,296 rows. Chest measurements remain complementary; they are not pooled with the primary near-eye measurements.

All participants with near-eye or chest metrics have matching participant characteristics, chronotype, LEBA, VLSQ-8, and sleep-diary inputs. Exercise diary data are absent for three participants at each placement, and light-exposure diary data are absent for one participant at each placement. These are availability flags, not exclusions at this point.

Current MDER in the shared inputs

The shared inputs use the current metric version. In these files, daily MDER is the arithmetic mean of viable one-minute melEDI/illuminance ratios on a complete 1,440-position local wall-clock grid. Both channels must be finite and strictly greater than zero; a zero-denominator ratio is undefined, and a zero in either channel does not define the positive proportional comparison used by this estimand. When the autumn clock change produces duplicate local minutes, the two channels are averaged separately within that clock minute before the ratio is formed. An absent spring-forward minute remains missing.

At least 720 viable ratios are required, and exactly 720 passes the inclusive 50% rule. Failing that rule makes MDER alone unavailable: the participant-day, its other metrics, and its attached participant and site context remain in the shared inputs. Current MDER uses no time-profile weighting, exclusion, or scaling and is not a ratio of daily integrals. The older ratio-of-integrals and profile-based exclusion records document an earlier method and are not active.

In the primary dataset, MDER is available for 702 of 816 near-eye participant-days from 137 participants (mean 0.7239; median 0.7239) and 732 of 902 chest participant-days from 152 participants (mean 0.7565; median 0.7494). In the gap-timing-unaware dataset, the same momentary-ratio estimand is available for 687 of 811 near-eye participant-days from 137 participants (mean 0.7243; median 0.7239) and 723 of 897 chest participant-days from 152 participants (mean 0.7567; median 0.7495).

The stored comparison found all 25,620 non-MDER participant-day cells exactly unchanged and retained all 618 × 47 site/daylight context values exactly. Thus no non-MDER or site-context scientific value changed.

Source-verified L10 zeros in the shared inputs

L10 is the mean melEDI during the darkest 10-hour window of a participant-day. Its geometric mean can represent true zeros because production adds 0.1 lx, averages log10(melEDI + 0.1), transforms back, and subtracts 0.1 lx. In exact arithmetic, an all-zero source window therefore returns zero. Floating-point arithmetic left eight primary L10 means at 4.163336342344337e-17 lx instead. This remainder is far below the source-verified tolerance of 2.220446049250313e-14 lx.

The active numerical-zero rule stores a positive remainder within that tolerance as exact zero only when every finite source minute is exactly zero. Missing minutes remain missing; they are counted separately and are never imputed as zeros. A small positive remainder is retained if any finite source minute is positive, and a materially negative result stops production.

Exactly eight primary L10 mean cells changed: three near-eye and five chest. Seven 600-minute windows contained 600 observed zero minutes. The remaining chest window contained 547 observed zero minutes and 53 missing minutes; all 547 observed values were zero, while the 53 missing minutes stayed missing. All 816 near-eye and 902 chest participant-days remain in the shared inputs.

Every non-L10 scientific value, every sample definition, all 30-minute and hourly values, and all gap-timing-unaware scientific values are unchanged. The shared primary inputs inherit the eight exact-zero L10 values without a sample change. No hypothesis model was fitted or refitted.

For an analysis that permits zero outcomes, these participant-days remain as zeros. In a two-part analysis, they remain in the zero/occurrence component and are excluded only from the strictly positive magnitude component by that component’s definition.

6. Store the first hypothesis-specific inputs

Once the H01 method was approved, the shared inputs and H01 metric contract were used to store outcomes, site and daylight predictors, participant identifiers, metric-specific inclusion records, and sample flow. The same H01 implementation contract was applied separately to the gap-timing-unaware dataset. Neither operation fits a model.

H01 covers 17 metric contracts. The following tables report the all-available near-eye prepared frames. “Rows in prepared frame” is the potential input at this boundary, not a claim about the final fitted-model sample. Any later transformation or model-specific missing-value removal is rechecked and stored by the H01 analysis. Valid measurement hours describe the support from which a metric was derived; they are not additional model observations.

For the brightest and darkest 10-hour means and their midpoints, the primary metric files do not export the exact contributing minutes; the corresponding hour cells remain unavailable. The gap-timing-unaware artifacts do not retain exact metric-support hours for any H01 metric, so the full column is shown as unavailable.

The H01 files also prepare comparisons using the same participants and participant-days at both sensor positions where possible. Interdaily stability and intradaily variability each produce one participant-level value from a series of days. Their paired versions would require recomputation from the same minute-level day set at both placements. They are therefore explicitly marked unavailable for both placements in both datasets—eight scenario records in total—instead of being approximated from unequal day sets.

check_evidence_manifest <- function(path, expected_sha256, family) {
  manifest <- read_stored_csv(path)
  assert_columns(
    manifest,
    c("path", "sha256", "bytes", "producer", "r_version", "status"),
    family
  )
  check_file_hash(path, expected_sha256)
  listed_paths <- absolute_path(manifest$path)
  present <- file.exists(listed_paths)
  actual_bytes <- rep(NA_real_, length(listed_paths))
  actual_sha256 <- rep(NA_character_, length(listed_paths))
  actual_bytes[present] <- file.info(listed_paths[present])$size
  actual_sha256[present] <- vapply(
    listed_paths[present],
    function(file) digest::digest(file = file, algo = "sha256"),
    character(1)
  )
  stopifnot(
    all(manifest$r_version == "4.6.1"),
    all(manifest$status == "PASS"),
    all(present),
    all(actual_bytes == manifest$bytes),

    data = manifest,

      stage = family,
      `Manifest SHA-256` = expected_sha256,

      `Size checks` = sum(actual_bytes == manifest$bytes),
      `SHA-256 checks` = sum(actual_sha256 == manifest$sha256),
      Check = "PASS"
    )
  )
}

summarise_grid <- function(data, prefix) {
  output <- data |>
    dplyr::group_by(.data$site) |>
    dplyr::summarise(
      participants = dplyr::n_distinct(.data$Id),
      participant_days = dplyr::n_distinct(
        paste(.data$Id, .data$local_date)
      ),
      grid_rows = dplyr::n(),
      admissible_rows = sum(.data$bin_admissible %in% TRUE),
      .groups = "drop"
    )
  names(output)[-1] <- paste(prefix, names(output)[-1], sep = "_")
  output
}

## 4. Preserve time of day and actual elapsed time

Local clock time and elapsed time answer different questions. A 30-minute row

intervals; its stored local-clock outcome is their average. When clocks move
forward, a local-clock interval can have no corresponding real interval.

sequence. A real gap, unusable outcome, repeated autumn interval, or

### Current MDER in the shared inputs

The shared inputs use the current metric version. In these files, daily MDER
is the arithmetic mean of viable one-minute melEDI/illuminance ratios on a complete
1,440-position local wall-clock grid. Both channels must be finite and
strictly greater than zero; a zero-denominator ratio is undefined, and a zero
in either channel does not define the positive proportional comparison used by
this estimand. When the autumn clock change produces duplicate local minutes,
the two channels are averaged separately within that clock minute before the
ratio is formed. An absent spring-forward minute remains missing.

At least 720 viable ratios are required, and exactly 720 passes the inclusive
50% rule. Failing that rule makes MDER alone unavailable: the participant-day,
its other metrics, and its attached participant and site context remain in the
shared inputs. Current MDER uses no time-profile weighting, exclusion, or
scaling and is not a ratio of daily integrals. The older ratio-of-integrals and
profile-based exclusion records document an earlier method and are not active.

### Source-verified L10 zeros in the shared inputs

L10 is the mean melEDI during the darkest 10-hour window of a participant-day.
Its geometric mean can represent true zeros because production adds 0.1 lx,
averages `log10(melEDI + 0.1)`, transforms back, and subtracts 0.1 lx. In exact
arithmetic, an all-zero source window therefore returns zero. Floating-point
arithmetic left eight primary L10 means at
`4.163336342344337e-17` lx instead. This remainder is far below the
source-verified tolerance of `2.220446049250313e-14` lx.

The active numerical-zero rule stores a positive remainder within that
tolerance as exact zero only when every finite source minute is exactly zero.
Missing minutes remain missing; they are counted separately and are never
imputed as zeros. A small positive remainder is retained if any finite source
minute is positive, and a materially negative result stops production.
