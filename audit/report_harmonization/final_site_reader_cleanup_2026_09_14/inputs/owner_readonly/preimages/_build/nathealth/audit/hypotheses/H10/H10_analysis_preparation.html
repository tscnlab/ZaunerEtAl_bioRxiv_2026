From verified demographic and personal light-exposure records to reported associations

Last modified:

August 22, 2026

Purpose

NoteAbout this analysis record

This page explains how verified demographic data and personal light-exposure metrics were turned into the H10 results. The companion H10 results report presents the scientific findings.

Model estimates and intervals, model checks, influence results, sensitivity analyses, figures, and source data are read from stored H10 outputs. This page does not fit or compare models, calculate predictions, repeat influence checks, bootstrap, resample, or simulate data.

The preregistered hypothesis was:

Personal light exposure metrics depend on age and gender.

Biological sex and gender were recorded as separate variables. The accepted analyses used biological sex, coded Female or Male; gender was not analysed. Age associations were estimated per 10-year increase and describe cross-sectional associations, not causal effects of ageing. The primary estimands were the site-adjusted common age association and Female-minus-Male association. Separate age-by-site and biological-sex-by-site interactions allowed those associations to differ by study site.

The primary near-eye sensor position sampled light closer to the eyes during wear, although it did not directly measure retinal exposure. The complementary chest sensor position measured light at the chest, not ocular exposure. The 17 responses include melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI), an illuminance weighted for melanopsin-related sensitivity. A participant-day is one participant’s retained daily record on one local calendar day.

Every reported interval is a 95% confidence interval (95% CI). Four separate complete 17-test families use false-discovery-rate (FDR) adjustment: age main effects, biological-sex main effects, age-by-site interactions, and biological-sex-by-site interactions. Metric derivation and support are documented in Preparation 04, and model-ready dataset construction is documented in Preparation 06. The H10 results report presents the scientific findings and its linked registration-record summary.

Verified inputs, demographic construct, and response contracts

Verified inputs and current qualification

The analysis used the current Preparation 06 model-ready layer. Its base objects contained 816 near-eye and 902 chest participant-days before metric-specific availability was applied. The model-ready layer, current MDER values, and L10 numerical-zero normalization were independently verified. The L10 normalization changed exactly eight primary L10 values from numerical noise to exact zero without changing a sample, a non-L10 scientific value, or an inferential decision. The corrected gap-timing-unaware chest day with no viable momentary ratio is recorded as missing, and every finite gap MDER value is strictly positive.

The remaining qualification is explicit: complete independent reconstruction of the exact current state-support classification remains open. This qualification is not evidence that the stored values are incorrect.

Demographic construct and site support

Age and biological sex were joined as participant-level variables and were complete in every fitted frame. Biological sex had exactly the levels Male and Female. Male was the reference, so the reported coefficient is Female minus Male. Site was explicitly sum-coded. The gender variable and unsupported predictors did not enter any model.

The smallest fitted site-by-sex cell was the near-eye San José (CR) Male cell with two participants. This was estimable but motivated the interaction and leave-one-site-out qualifications.

For METRIC-010, daily MDER is the arithmetic mean of viable one-minute melEDI/illuminance ratios on a complete 1,440-minute local wall-clock grid. A minute is viable only when both channels are finite and strictly positive, and the daily value is retained with at least 720 viable minutes. Fall-back duplicate local minutes are averaged channel-wise before forming the ratio; spring-forward absent minutes remain missing. The analysis does not use time-profile weighting or a ratio of daily integrals.

Model construction and inference

Exact Wilkinson formulae

For participant-day outcomes, the random effect (1 | site:Id) accounts for repeated days within participant and represents remaining between-participant variation after the fixed predictors and study site are considered. IS and IV have one row per participant, so an ordinary site-adjusted linear model avoids a non-identifiable one-observation random effect. The exact evaluated formulae stored with the model records are:

Age was represented as age_decade = age / 10; this changes the coefficient’s unit but not fitted values or test statistics. Its coefficient is the cross-sectional association per 10-year increase, not a causal ageing effect. Site used sum contrasts. Biological sex used Male then Female treatment coding. The additive coefficient was the site-adjusted common association. An interaction comparison asked separately whether the association varied among sites. Where an interaction summary uses a site-average estimate, it is an average across the represented sites that gives each site equal weight.

Engines, estimation, and confidence intervals

Gaussian participant-day comparisons were fitted by maximum likelihood and their final coefficients by REML. Gaussian participant-level models used ordinary least squares. Tweedie/log models used maximum likelihood. The model record contains 612 fits: five comparison fits and four final fits for each of 68 frames.

Every reported coefficient has a model-based 95% CI. Ordinary linear-model coefficients use a residual-degree-of-freedom t interval; mixed-model and Tweedie coefficients use the fixed-effect covariance matrix with a normal critical value. Logit effects are odds ratios, base-10 transformed Gaussian effects are ratios, Tweedie/log effects are ratios, and identity-scale effects are differences. Clock-time differences are reported in minutes. Back-transformed effects have been converted from the model scale to these practical ratios or differences.

Model checks, influence, and limitations

All 612 fits converged, had positive-definite Hessians and full-rank fixed effects, were non-singular, and emitted no fit error. Model checks were interpreted for 68 primary metric-sensor-position-association targets: 25 were acceptable and 43 acceptable with specified limitations; none was classified not acceptable.

  }

  }
  output
}

## Purpose

::: {.callout-note title="About this analysis record"}
This page explains how verified demographic data and personal light-exposure
metrics were turned into the H10 results. The companion
[H10 results report](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H10.qmd) presents the
scientific findings.

Model estimates and intervals, model checks, influence results, sensitivity
analyses, figures, and source data are read from stored H10 outputs. This page
does not fit or compare models, calculate predictions, repeat influence checks,
bootstrap, resample, or simulate data.
:::

The preregistered hypothesis was:

> Personal light exposure metrics depend on age and gender.

Biological sex and gender were recorded as separate variables.
The accepted analyses used biological sex, coded Female or Male; gender was not analysed.
Age associations were estimated per 10-year increase and describe cross-sectional associations, not
causal effects of ageing. The primary estimands were the site-adjusted common
age association and Female-minus-Male association. Separate age-by-site and
biological-sex-by-site interactions allowed those associations to differ by
study site.

The primary near-eye sensor position sampled light closer to the eyes during
wear, although it did not directly measure retinal exposure. The complementary
chest sensor position measured light at the chest, not ocular exposure. The 17
responses include melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI), an
illuminance weighted for melanopsin-related sensitivity. A participant-day is
one participant's retained daily record on one local calendar day.

Every reported interval is a 95% confidence interval (95% CI). Four separate
complete 17-test families use false-discovery-rate (FDR) adjustment: age main
effects, biological-sex main effects, age-by-site interactions, and
biological-sex-by-site interactions. Metric derivation and support are
documented in [Preparation 04](../../../notebooks/preparation/04_metric_derivation.qmd),
and model-ready dataset construction is documented in
[Preparation 06](../../../notebooks/preparation/06_model_ready_datasets.qmd).
The [H10 results report](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H10.qmd) presents the
scientific findings and its
[linked registration-record summary](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H10.qmd#h10-preregistration-deviations).

## Model construction and inference

### Exact Wilkinson formulae

For participant-day outcomes, the random effect `(1 | site:Id)` accounts for
repeated days within participant and represents remaining between-participant
variation after the fixed predictors and study site are considered. IS and IV
have one row per participant, so an ordinary site-adjusted linear model avoids
a non-identifiable one-observation random effect. The exact evaluated formulae
stored with the model records are:

Age was represented as `age_decade = age / 10`; this changes the coefficient's
unit but not fitted values or test statistics. Its coefficient is the
cross-sectional association per 10-year increase, not a causal ageing effect.
Site used sum contrasts. Biological sex used Male then Female treatment coding.
The additive coefficient was the site-adjusted common association. An
interaction comparison asked separately whether the association varied among
sites. Where an interaction summary uses a **site-average estimate**, it is an
average across the represented sites that gives each site equal weight.

Gaussian participant-day comparisons were fitted by maximum likelihood and
their final coefficients by REML. Gaussian participant-level models used
ordinary least squares. Tweedie/log models used maximum likelihood. The model
record contains 612 fits: five comparison fits and four final fits for each of
68 frames.
