From verified chronotype fields and personal light-exposure timing metrics to reported associations

Last modified:

August 22, 2026

Purpose

This page explains how verified chronotype fields and personal light-exposure timing metrics lead to the scientific findings in the H09 results report.

H09 asks whether participant-level chronotype is associated with five participant-day timing metrics of personal light exposure. A participant-day is one participant’s eligible outcome on one local date. Chronotype is represented by the Munich Chronotype Questionnaire corrected midsleep on free days (MCTQ MSFsc) and the Morningness–Eveningness Questionnaire (MEQ). Larger MCTQ MSFsc means later corrected midsleep, whereas larger MEQ means greater morning preference; the two constructs remain separate in the models and inferential families.

Personal light exposure is expressed as melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI). The primary near-eye sensor position records light near the eyes during wear. The complementary chest sensor position is analysed separately, is not pooled with near-eye measurement, and is not a measure of ocular exposure.

The gap-timing-unaware dataset is the dataset applying the 50%-per-hour and 80%-per-day coverage rules but not using the remaining gaps’ time of day in metric-specific support decisions. At this first explanation it can be contrasted with the time-sensitive primary dataset; hereafter the latter is called simply the primary dataset.

This page identifies the exact inputs, derived fields, analytical frames, formulae, model checks, sensitivity analyses, outputs, and file identities behind the results. It documents provenance and does not create an additional analysis. Uncertainty is reported as 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs), and multiplicity is handled with false-discovery-rate (FDR) adjustment.

Centering and scaling

The two model-column names, mctq_hour_centered and meq_10_centered, encode only a change of origin and, for MEQ, a reporting scale:

  • MCTQ: mctq_hour_centered = MCTQ MSFsc in hours - 4.1135843
  • MEQ: meq_10_centered = (MEQ score - 52.8602151) / 10

Thus one MCTQ unit means one hour later corrected midsleep, while one MEQ unit means 10 score points toward greater morning preference. A centered value of zero is the observed participant mean. Centering changes the intercept’s reference point; it does not change the chronotype slope, its 95% CI, or its test.

Timing metrics and analytical samples

Metric definitions

Five registered participant-day outcomes enter each confirmatory family: the midpoint of the brightest 10 hours (M10 midpoint), midpoint of the darkest 10 hours (L10 midpoint), first and last time above 250 lx melEDI, and midpoint of the longest continuous period above 250 lx melEDI. The longest-period midpoint is calculated only when the selected period is exactly identifiable from its recorded endpoints. Mean timing above 250 lx melEDI is a distinct sensitivity estimand and is not substituted for the registered midpoint. M10 and L10 onset and offset are excluded. Preparation 04 documents metric derivation, and Preparation 06 documents the model-ready fields and support.

Clock values were linearized because their observed support was adequately linear. Night-time values use the specified negative-hour conversion where applicable; no circular response distribution was imposed merely because an outcome is clock-valued.

Models, estimands, uncertainty, and multiplicity

Exact formulae supplied to the models

For each metric, placement, dataset, sample, and chronotype instrument, the site-only model is compared with an additive chronotype model. The additive model estimates the study-site-adjusted average association and includes a participant random effect, which represents remaining between-participant timing variation after site and chronotype are considered. A second comparison adds a chronotype-by-site interaction, allowing the chronotype association to differ by study site. The distinction between these two questions is recorded in DEV-038.

Each coefficient is a signed effect in local clock hours. Positive values mean the timing outcome occurs later per one-hour later MCTQ MSFsc or per 10 points greater MEQ morning preference; negative values mean earlier. Restricted maximum-likelihood additive fits provide coefficients and 95% Wald CIs; maximum-likelihood fits provide the nested likelihood-ratio tests. A natural-spline fit is used only to assess whether the observed predictor support is adequately represented as linear.

Multiplicity families

FDR adjustment is applied in four separate five-outcome families: MCTQ average associations, MEQ average associations, MCTQ-by-site interactions, and MEQ-by-site interactions. Near-eye, chest, paired, and preparation-sensitivity runs retain separate family instances. Significance is decided from unrounded adjusted values before three-decimal display. Site-specific slopes are descriptive unless the global chronotype-by-site interaction family supports heterogeneity. The complete-family choice is recorded in DEV-039; the H09 results deviation section summarizes all three current scientific deviations.

## Purpose

This page explains how verified chronotype fields and personal light-exposure
timing metrics lead to the scientific findings in the [H09 results
report](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H09.qmd).

H09 asks whether participant-level chronotype is associated with five
participant-day timing metrics of personal light exposure. A participant-day
is one participant's eligible outcome on one local date. Chronotype is
represented by the **Munich Chronotype Questionnaire corrected midsleep on
free days (MCTQ MSFsc)** and the **Morningness–Eveningness Questionnaire
(MEQ)**. Larger MCTQ MSFsc means later corrected midsleep, whereas larger MEQ
means greater morning preference; the two constructs remain separate in the
models and inferential families.

Personal light exposure is expressed as **melanopic equivalent daylight
illuminance (melEDI)**. The primary near-eye sensor position records light near
the eyes during wear. The complementary chest sensor position is analysed
separately, is not pooled with near-eye measurement, and is not a measure of
ocular exposure.

The **gap-timing-unaware dataset** is the dataset applying the 50%-per-hour
and 80%-per-day coverage rules but not using the remaining gaps' time of day
in metric-specific support decisions. At this first explanation it can be
contrasted with the time-sensitive primary dataset; hereafter the latter is
called simply the primary dataset.

This page identifies the exact inputs, derived fields, analytical frames,
formulae, model checks, sensitivity analyses, outputs, and file identities
behind the results. It documents provenance and does not create an additional
analysis. Uncertainty is reported as 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs), and
multiplicity is handled with false-discovery-rate (FDR) adjustment.

### Centering and scaling

The two model-column names, `mctq_hour_centered` and `meq_10_centered`, encode
only a change of origin and, for MEQ, a reporting scale:

- **MCTQ:** `mctq_hour_centered = MCTQ MSFsc in hours - 4.1135843`
- **MEQ:** `meq_10_centered = (MEQ score - 52.8602151) / 10`

Thus one MCTQ unit means one hour later corrected midsleep, while one MEQ unit
means 10 score points toward greater morning preference. A centered value of
zero is the observed participant mean. Centering changes the intercept's
reference point; it does not change the chronotype slope, its 95% CI, or its
test.

## Models, estimands, uncertainty, and multiplicity

### Exact formulae supplied to the models

For each metric, placement, dataset, sample, and chronotype instrument, the
site-only model is compared with an additive chronotype model. The additive
model estimates the study-site-adjusted average association and includes a
participant random effect, which represents remaining between-participant
timing variation after site and chronotype are considered. A second comparison
adds a chronotype-by-site interaction, allowing the chronotype association to
differ by study site. The distinction between these two questions is recorded
in [`DEV-038`](../../../notebooks/preregistration_deviations.qmd#dev-038).

Each coefficient is a signed effect in local clock hours. Positive values mean
the timing outcome occurs later per one-hour later MCTQ MSFsc or per 10 points
greater MEQ morning preference; negative values mean earlier. Restricted
maximum-likelihood additive fits provide coefficients and 95% Wald CIs;
maximum-likelihood fits provide the nested likelihood-ratio tests. A
natural-spline fit is used only to assess whether the observed predictor
support is adequately represented as linear.
