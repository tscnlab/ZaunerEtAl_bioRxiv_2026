From verified light-exposure metrics and questionnaire scores to reported associations

Last modified:

August 20, 2026

About this analysis record

NoteAbout this analysis record

This page explains how verified light-exposure metrics and questionnaire scores were turned into the H05 results. Rendering it recalculates only file-identity, schema, and key checks plus lightweight descriptive summaries from the stored H05 model frames. It reads fitted models, estimates, intervals, model checks, influence screens, and predefined alternative analyses from their recorded files. It does not fit or refit a model, calculate predictions, repeat model checks, rerun leave-one-site-out analyses, bootstrap, or simulate data.

H05 asks whether four Light Exposure Behaviour Assessment (LEBA) questionnaire factors are associated with 17 personal light-exposure metrics. The primary analysis uses the near-eye sensor position, which measures light close to the eyes. All-available chest measurements provide complementary evidence but are not interpreted as ocular exposure. A participant-day is one eligible local calendar day from one participant; participant-day metrics can therefore include repeated days from the same person.

Factor effects are reported per one participant-level standard deviation (SD) of the score, with a 95% confidence interval (95% CI). Each inferential placement or dataset applies false-discovery-rate (FDR) adjustment to the complete family of four factors × 17 metrics = 68 tests. Participant-level and participant-day metrics use different model structures but share the same fixed-site adjustment, which estimates one adjustment coefficient for each study site, as well as the same questionnaire-score scaling and family definition.

This page identifies the data and code that produced those results, explains why each analytical operation was necessary, and shows the stored evidence used to check the analysis. Read it alongside the H05 results report; the exact H05 linked registration-record summary is mapped there once.

2. Metric construction and analytical samples

Coverage, gaps, and metric support

The primary dataset applied the general requirement of at least 50% valid minutes per hour and at least 80% valid hours per day, together with metric-specific support rules where the timing of remaining gaps affects what the metric can represent. Unsupported values remained missing rather than being interpreted as darkness. Light-level metrics use melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI) in reader-facing labels, with a 0.1 lx offset where a logarithmic transform requires it. A Tweedie model is a positive-response regression model used here for non-negative, right-skewed durations while retaining exact zeros.

A sensitivity analysis repeats the accepted comparison under a predefined alternative to assess whether the interpretation is stable. One predefined sensitivity uses the gap-timing-unaware dataset. It still passed the general 50%-per-hour and 80%-per-day coverage rules, but the timing of the remaining missing observations was not used for an additional metric-specific adjustment. For contrast at this first explanation, the primary dataset could be interpreted as a time-sensitive primary metric dataset. Hereafter it is called simply the primary dataset.

MDER is the arithmetic mean of viable one-minute melEDI/illuminance ratios. Both channels must be finite and strictly positive, and at least 720 of the complete 1,440 local wall-clock minutes must provide a viable ratio. Repeated local minutes at daylight-saving fall-back are averaged channel-wise before forming the ratio; absent spring-forward minutes remain missing. Each viable minute ratio has equal weight: there is no time-profile weighting, gating, or scaling, and the daily value is not a ratio of daily integrals. Failure of this support rule makes only MDER missing; it does not delete the day from other metric analyses.

The longest continuous period above 250 lx melEDI is an observed lower bound when a gap can interrupt the period. Its exact-identifiability sensitivity therefore retains only participant-days for which the longest period is fully observed. The L10 midpoint retains its continuous range and its strict-after-16:00 representation; no noon sensitivity was needed.

The shared derivation rules are documented in Preparation 04, and the model-ready datasets are documented in Preparation 06.

Model-frame construction

h05_prepare_metric_rows() selects one placement, preparation, sample rule, and metric at a time. It applies metric eligibility, checks the participant or participant-day key, joins LEBA by site and participant, excludes rows without a complete factor score, fixes the registered site order, and sorts rows by site, participant, and local date. Interdaily stability and intradaily variability contribute one row per participant. The other 15 metrics contribute one row per eligible participant-day.

The archive contains 136 run-by-metric frames (8 × 17). Joining the four LEBA factors creates the 544 model-index rows without changing a metric frame’s participants or observations.

3. Models, estimands, and inference

Estimand, practical effects, uncertainty, and multiplicity

For each metric and factor, the full model adds the centred LEBA score to the reduced site-adjusted model. A likelihood-ratio comparison asks whether the full model describes the data better than the reduced model on a common likelihood basis. The raw inferential p-value comes from that comparison. Each declared family contains all 17 metrics × 4 factors = 68 tests, and FDR adjustment is performed on the complete 68-value vector. Support is retained at adjusted p ≤ 0.050, decided before display formatting.

Back-transformation converts a fitted-scale coefficient into the practical response scale used for interpretation. Coefficients are back-transformed and reported per one participant-level LEBA SD. Gaussian identity outcomes return a difference in the stated metric unit, log-transformed and Tweedie outcomes return a ratio, IS returns an odds ratio, and clock-time outcomes return a difference in hours. Every reported 95% CI is a two-sided Wald interval from the final model. No secondary significance screen is attached to descriptive sensitivities.

NoteWhy a less conservative adjustment can accompany fewer retained associations

For one unchanged vector of raw p-values, the complete-vector FDR adjustment used here cannot retain fewer associations than Bonferroni at the same 0.050 threshold. The H05 family above is based on likelihood-ratio p-values from the fixed-site models. It is therefore not interchangeable with a family built from unadjusted rank correlations or another raw test. Multiplicity adjustment and the construction of the raw p-values are separate analytical decisions; the latter can change which associations enter the smallest ranks before either correction is applied.

Site specification

A fixed site effect gives each study site its own adjustment coefficient. A random site effect instead represents between-site variation through one estimated variance component. The fixed-site primary specification differs from the registered random-site structure. The decision was based on the inherited 17-metric site-structure evidence: every fixed-site model converged with a positive-definite Hessian and none was singular, whereas the random-site descriptions contained unstable, non-estimable, and singular fits. Keeping site fixed also estimates each site’s adjustment directly rather than relying on a variance component from only nine sites. This is an adjustment for observed site differences, not a claim that site causes an exposure outcome. The random-site structure remains a sensitivity.

6. Current numerical-zero and MDER qualifications

Numerical-zero normalization for darkest-10-hour mean melEDI

The current rule sets a geometric-mean back-transformation residual to exact zero only when it lies within the unit-aware floating-point tolerance and every finite source minute entering the selected window is exactly zero. For these L10 windows, the tolerance is 2.220446049250313 × 10-14 lx. Missing source minutes remain missing and are never imputed as zero.

Under this rule, three primary near-eye and five primary chest participant-days are exact zero rather than approximately 4.163336342344337 × 10-17 lx. This does not change a fitted sample, any non-L10 value, hourly or 30-minute value, participant-level value, or site/daylight-context value. The gap-timing-unaware L10 values were already exact zeros.

How to examine or reproduce H05

A reader can follow the analysis in five passes:

  1. inspect H05_input_audit.csv, h05_contract.R, and the factor, metric, formula, and run registries to verify the frozen inputs and specifications;
  2. compare the exact samples above with H05_model_frame_index.csv and the 136 frames in H05_model_frames.rds;
  3. follow run_h05_stage2.R through h05_modeling.R, then the bounded MDER and L10 reseal scripts, to connect each frame to its current model object, coefficient, interval, likelihood-ratio test, diagnostic, and sensitivity record;
  4. inspect the complete 68-value family audit before interpreting any individual association; and
  5. read the H05 results report alongside its tables, figures, and paired source data.

The most important boundary is that the preparation record documents a frozen analysis; it does not create an alternate result. If an input, metric rule, response model, or estimand changes, the affected scientific pipeline must be rerun before this page is refreshed.

## About this analysis record

::: {.callout-note title="About this analysis record"}
This page explains how verified light-exposure metrics and questionnaire scores
were turned into the H05 results. Rendering it recalculates only file-identity,
schema, and key checks plus lightweight descriptive summaries from the stored
H05 model frames. It reads fitted models, estimates, intervals, model checks,
influence screens, and predefined alternative analyses from their recorded files. It does
not fit or refit a model, calculate predictions, repeat model checks, rerun
leave-one-site-out analyses, bootstrap, or simulate data.
:::

H05 asks whether four Light Exposure Behaviour Assessment (LEBA) questionnaire
factors are associated with 17 personal light-exposure metrics. The primary
analysis uses the near-eye sensor position, which measures light close to the
eyes. All-available chest measurements provide complementary evidence but are
not interpreted as ocular exposure. A **participant-day** is one eligible
local calendar day from one participant; participant-day metrics can therefore
include repeated days from the same person.

Factor effects are reported per one participant-level standard deviation (SD)
of the score, with a 95% confidence interval (95% CI). Each inferential
placement or dataset applies false-discovery-rate (FDR) adjustment to the
complete family of four factors × 17 metrics = 68 tests. Participant-level and
participant-day metrics use different model structures but share the same
fixed-site adjustment, which estimates one adjustment coefficient for each
study site, as well as the same questionnaire-score scaling and family
definition.

This page identifies the data and code that produced those results, explains
why each analytical operation was necessary, and shows the stored evidence
used to check the analysis. Read it alongside the
[H05 results report](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H05.qmd); the exact
[H05 linked registration-record summary](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H05.qmd#h05-preregistration-deviations)
is mapped there once.

## 2. Metric construction and analytical samples

### Coverage, gaps, and metric support

The primary dataset applied the general requirement of at least 50% valid
minutes per hour and at least 80% valid hours per day, together with
metric-specific support rules where the timing of remaining gaps affects what
the metric can represent. Unsupported values remained missing rather than
being interpreted as darkness. Light-level metrics use melanopic equivalent
daylight illuminance (melEDI) in reader-facing labels, with a 0.1 lx offset
where a logarithmic transform requires it. A Tweedie model is a positive-response
regression model used here for non-negative, right-skewed durations while
retaining exact zeros.

A sensitivity analysis repeats the accepted comparison under a predefined
alternative to assess whether the interpretation is stable. One predefined
sensitivity uses the **gap-timing-unaware dataset**. It still
passed the general 50%-per-hour and 80%-per-day coverage rules, but the timing
of the remaining missing observations was not used for an additional
metric-specific adjustment. For contrast at this first explanation, the
primary dataset could be interpreted as a **time-sensitive primary metric
dataset**. Hereafter it is called simply the primary dataset.

MDER is the arithmetic mean of viable one-minute melEDI/illuminance ratios.
Both channels must be finite and strictly positive, and at least 720 of the
complete 1,440 local wall-clock minutes must provide a viable ratio. Repeated
local minutes at daylight-saving fall-back are averaged channel-wise before
forming the ratio; absent spring-forward minutes remain missing. Each viable
minute ratio has equal weight: there is no time-profile weighting, gating, or
scaling, and the daily value is not a ratio of daily integrals. Failure of this
support rule makes only MDER missing; it does not delete the day from other
metric analyses.

The longest continuous period above 250 lx melEDI is an observed lower bound
when a gap can interrupt the period. Its exact-identifiability sensitivity
therefore retains only participant-days for which the longest **period** is
fully observed. The L10 midpoint retains its continuous range and its
strict-after-16:00 representation; no noon sensitivity was needed.

[Preparation 04](../../../notebooks/preparation/04_metric_derivation.qmd), and

## 3. Models, estimands, and inference

### Estimand, practical effects, uncertainty, and multiplicity

For each metric and factor, the full model adds the centred LEBA score to the
reduced site-adjusted model. A likelihood-ratio comparison asks whether the
full model describes the data better than the reduced model on a common
likelihood basis. The raw inferential *p*-value comes from that comparison. Each declared family contains
all 17 metrics × 4 factors = 68 tests, and FDR adjustment is performed on the
complete 68-value vector. Support is retained at adjusted *p* ≤ 0.050, decided
before display formatting.

Back-transformation converts a fitted-scale coefficient into the practical
response scale used for interpretation. Coefficients are back-transformed and
reported per one participant-level LEBA SD. Gaussian identity outcomes return a difference in
the stated metric unit, log-transformed and Tweedie outcomes return a ratio,
IS returns an odds ratio, and clock-time outcomes return a difference in
hours. Every reported 95% CI is a two-sided Wald interval from the final model.
No secondary significance screen is attached to descriptive sensitivities.

::: {.callout-note title="Why a less conservative adjustment can accompany fewer retained associations"}
For one unchanged vector of raw *p*-values, the complete-vector FDR adjustment
used here cannot retain fewer associations than Bonferroni at the same 0.050
threshold. The H05 family above is based on likelihood-ratio *p*-values from
the fixed-site models. It is therefore not interchangeable with a family built
from unadjusted rank correlations or another raw test. Multiplicity adjustment
and the construction of the raw *p*-values are separate analytical decisions;
the latter can change which associations enter the smallest ranks before
either correction is applied.
:::

### Site specification

A fixed site effect gives each study site its own adjustment coefficient. A
random site effect instead represents between-site variation through one
estimated variance component. The fixed-site primary specification differs
from the registered random-site structure. The decision was based on the inherited 17-metric site-structure
evidence: every fixed-site model converged with a positive-definite Hessian and
none was singular, whereas the random-site descriptions contained unstable,
non-estimable, and singular fits. Keeping site fixed also estimates each
site's adjustment directly rather than relying on a variance component from
only nine sites. This is an adjustment for observed site differences, not a
claim that site causes an exposure outcome. The random-site structure remains
a sensitivity.

## 6. Current numerical-zero and MDER qualifications

### Numerical-zero normalization for darkest-10-hour mean melEDI

The current rule sets a geometric-mean back-transformation residual to exact
zero only when it lies within the unit-aware floating-point tolerance and
every finite source
minute entering the selected window is exactly zero. For these L10 windows,
the tolerance is 2.220446049250313 × 10^-14^ lx. Missing source minutes remain
missing and are never imputed as zero.

Under this rule, three primary near-eye and five primary chest
participant-days are exact zero rather than approximately
4.163336342344337 × 10^-17^ lx. This does not change a fitted sample, any
non-L10 value, hourly or 30-minute value, participant-level value, or
site/daylight-context value. The gap-timing-unaware L10 values were already
exact zeros.

### How to examine or reproduce H05

A reader can follow the analysis in five passes:

1. inspect `H05_input_audit.csv`, `h05_contract.R`, and the factor, metric,
   formula, and run registries to verify the frozen inputs and specifications;
2. compare the exact samples above with `H05_model_frame_index.csv` and the
   136 frames in `H05_model_frames.rds`;
3. follow `run_h05_stage2.R` through `h05_modeling.R`, then the bounded MDER
   and L10 reseal scripts, to connect each frame to its current model object,
   coefficient, interval, likelihood-ratio test, diagnostic, and sensitivity
   record;
4. inspect the complete 68-value family audit before interpreting any
   individual association; and
5. read the [H05 results report](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H05.qmd)
   alongside its tables, figures, and paired source data.

The most important boundary is that the preparation record documents a frozen
analysis; it does not create an alternate result. If an input, metric rule,
response model, or estimand changes, the affected scientific pipeline must be
rerun before this page is refreshed.
