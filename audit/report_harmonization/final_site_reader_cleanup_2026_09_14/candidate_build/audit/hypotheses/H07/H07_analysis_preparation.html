From verified participant-day metric frames to nonlinear photoperiod curves and qualifying transitions

Last modified:

August 12, 2026

Scientific target

This companion documents the analysis underlying the H07 results report. The result report’s preregistration-deviation section provides reciprocal navigation to the current scientific deviations.

The preregistered hypothesis was:

“H7: There is a ceiling (nonlinear) effect of absolute latitude and photoperiod on level-, duration-, and exposure-history-based light metrics.”

Melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI) is an illuminance-like measure weighted for melanopic photoreception and expressed in lux (lx). The primary near-eye sensor position places the wearable sensor near the eyes; the complementary chest sensor position is torso-worn and is not ocular exposure. A participant-day is one participant’s metric value on one local calendar date at one sensor position.

A nonlinear generalized additive model (GAM) analysis allows the photoperiod association to bend rather than follow a straight line. The derivative is the slope of the fitted curve: the change in the fitted outcome for each additional hour of photoperiod at each point. H07 evaluates it on the model’s linear-predictor scale, in model-scale units per hour. A pointwise 95% confidence interval (95% CI) describes uncertainty at one photoperiod value; it is not a simultaneous band for the whole curve. Overlap among nonlinear model terms makes their separate contributions harder to distinguish; the technical term for this is concurvity.

The design cannot separate absolute latitude from site, because each site has one latitude. The estimable target is therefore narrower: for each of nine planned metrics and each placement, describe the pooled association with civil photoperiod and ask whether the fitted smooth changes from a pointwise detected increase to a first derivative whose pointwise interval contains zero at every remaining grid point through the longest recorded photoperiod. This is a qualifying transition from increase to a sustained near-flat tail.1 It is not an equivalence test and does not establish a physiological or environmental ceiling.

Current preregistration deviations

  • DEV-016: the registered latitude-by-photoperiod surface is non-identifiable here, so the accepted report retains all nine outcomes and presents a separately labelled site-adjusted nonlinear photoperiod analysis as descriptive evidence.
  • DEV-033: all nine eligible outcomes remain visible rather than being selected using H01 significance or H07 AIC.
  • DEV-034: response specifications and model checks are metric-specific rather than one universal zero-aware log-Gaussian model.

Frame-integrity source data

No frame is selected using a fitted result from another hypothesis. Missing metric values are removed within the relevant metric and placement, so the sample is metric-specific. Civil photoperiod and latitude must be finite. Temperature is not part of the contract and is not introduced.

Candidate samples, exclusions, and support

The gap-timing-unaware dataset is a predefined sensitivity in which the timing of remaining missing observations is not used for an additional metric-specific correction. The general 50%-per-hour and 80%-per-day coverage rules still apply, so gaps and missingness are not ignored. For this contrast, the primary preparation could be interpreted as a time-sensitive primary metric dataset because it uses gap timing where that timing is relevant to a metric. Below, the main input is called simply the primary dataset.

Site, photoperiod, latitude, and collection period

Absolute latitude is constant within every site in every primary frame. It is therefore structurally inseparable from site. Civil photoperiod varies within site over collection dates, but the site ranges overlap only partially and collection periods differ by site. The pooled smooth can describe a visible association in the combined data; it cannot isolate photoperiod from every site-specific, seasonal, or collection-period difference.

Temporal order and repeated participant-days

Dates are local calendar dates. Civil photoperiod is joined by site and local date. Frames are ordered by site, participant, and date before fitting. Participants contribute repeated days, so each model contains a random-effect smooth for participant nested within site. Site also has its own random-effect smooth. One participant-day contributes only one row per metric and placement; there is no within-day residual time series in H07.

No independent temperature, season, or collection-period predictor is added. Season and collection period remain embedded in the observed date and photoperiod pattern. Consecutive-day residual correlations are descriptive checks of remaining temporal dependence rather than a fitted autocorrelation structure.

Association and derivative estimands

The association estimand is the fitted pooled civil-photoperiod smooth for one metric and placement, conditional on the model structure. Response-scale curves are used for scientific display: when a response was transformed for fitting, the fitted quantity was converted back to the displayed metric unit. The derivative estimand underlying the qualifying transition is the first derivative of that smooth on the model’s linear-predictor scale per hour of civil photoperiod.

For each metric and placement, derivatives are evaluated at 100 equally spaced points from the exact minimum to maximum recorded photoperiod. Central finite differences use an increment of 0.01 h; the lower boundary uses a forward difference and the upper boundary a backward difference. The pointwise 95% interval uses the unconditional covariance matrix.

A pattern is present only when:

  1. the immediately preceding grid point has a derivative interval wholly above zero;
  2. the next point has a derivative interval containing zero; and
  3. every later point through the recorded maximum also has an interval containing zero.

The transition is bracketed by the preceding and current grid points. A non-significant derivative at one point, a visual flattening, or an AIC threshold is not sufficient. The rule has no scientifically defensible equivalence margin, no simultaneous curve band, and no curve-wide or cross-metric multiplicity adjustment. It therefore supports only a pointwise, descriptive qualifying-transition classification, not a ceiling claim.

Model checks and support limits

All 18 reported photoperiod-smooth fits converged. Four smooths—three near-eye and one chest—met the recorded basis-dimension flag criterion, motivating the broader-basis sensitivity. Estimated overlap among nonlinear model terms (concurvity) supplied by the nested participant smooth to the photoperiod smooth was approximately 0.9985–0.9988. This is the principal identifiability limitation: the pooled photoperiod shape is difficult to separate from which participants contribute at which photoperiods.

Technical provenance and bounded reproduction

NoteExecution boundary

This companion reads frozen H07 inputs, participant-day model frames, fitted output summaries, model checks, figures, and manifests. ## Scientific target

This companion documents the analysis underlying the
[H07 results report](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H07.qmd). The result report's
[preregistration-deviation section](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H07.qmd#h07-preregistration-deviations)
provides reciprocal navigation to the current scientific deviations.

The preregistered hypothesis was:

> “H7: There is a ceiling (nonlinear) effect of absolute latitude and photoperiod on level-, duration-, and exposure-history-based light metrics.”

**Melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI)** is an illuminance-like
measure weighted for melanopic photoreception and expressed in lux (lx). The
**primary near-eye sensor position** places the wearable sensor near the eyes;
the **complementary chest sensor position** is torso-worn and is not ocular
exposure. A **participant-day** is one participant's metric value on one local
calendar date at one sensor position.

A **nonlinear generalized additive model (GAM) analysis** allows the
photoperiod association to bend rather than follow a straight line. The
**derivative** is the slope of the fitted curve: the change in the fitted
outcome for each additional hour of photoperiod at each point. H07 evaluates
it on the model's linear-predictor scale, in model-scale units per hour. A
**pointwise 95% confidence interval (95% CI)** describes uncertainty at one
photoperiod value; it is not a simultaneous band for the whole curve. **Overlap
among nonlinear model terms** makes their separate contributions harder to
distinguish; the technical term for this is *concurvity*.

The design cannot separate absolute latitude from site, because each site has
one latitude. The estimable target is therefore narrower: for each of nine
planned metrics and each placement, describe the pooled association with civil
photoperiod and ask whether the fitted smooth changes from a pointwise detected
increase to a first derivative whose pointwise interval contains zero at every
remaining grid point through the longest recorded photoperiod. This is a
qualifying transition from increase to a sustained near-flat tail.[^h07-prep-formal]
It is not an equivalence test and does not establish a physiological or
environmental ceiling.

[^h07-prep-formal]: The stored formal classification is
    **derivative-defined plateau pattern**. It is the three-part pointwise rule
    documented below, not a biological-ceiling, permanent-plateau, or
    equivalence claim.

### Association and derivative estimands

The association estimand is the fitted pooled civil-photoperiod smooth for one
metric and placement, conditional on the model structure. Response-scale
curves are used for scientific display: when a response was transformed for
fitting, the fitted quantity was converted back to the displayed metric unit.
The derivative estimand underlying the qualifying transition is the first
derivative of that smooth on the model's linear-predictor scale per hour of
civil photoperiod.

For each metric and placement, derivatives are evaluated at 100 equally spaced
points from the exact minimum to maximum recorded photoperiod. Central finite
differences use an increment of 0.01 h; the lower boundary uses a forward
difference and the upper boundary a backward difference. The pointwise 95%
interval uses the unconditional covariance matrix.

A pattern is present only when:

1. the immediately preceding grid point has a derivative interval wholly
   above zero;
2. the next point has a derivative interval containing zero; and
3. every later point through the recorded maximum also has an interval
   containing zero.

The transition is bracketed by the preceding and current grid points. A
non-significant derivative at one point, a visual flattening, or an AIC
threshold is not sufficient. The rule has no scientifically defensible
equivalence margin, no simultaneous curve band, and no curve-wide or
cross-metric multiplicity adjustment. It therefore supports only a
pointwise, descriptive qualifying-transition classification, not a ceiling
claim.
