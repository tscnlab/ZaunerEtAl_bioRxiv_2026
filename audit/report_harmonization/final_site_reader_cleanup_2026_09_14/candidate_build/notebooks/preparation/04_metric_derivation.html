Preparation 04: Derive light-exposure metrics

From eligible one-minute records to metric-specific analysis inputs

Last modified:

August 13, 2026

Purpose and position in the preparation chain

Preparation 04 converts the eligible one-minute light records from Preparation 02 into the daily, diary-period, 30-minute, hourly, and participant-level metrics used by later analyses. It also records whether each individual metric had enough relevant data to be interpreted. A failed support check makes only that metric unavailable; it does not discard the participant-day from unrelated analyses. For offset geometric means, it also records when floating-point roundoff is normalized to a source-verified exact zero.

The exact inputs are the Preparation 02 Rule A coverage files, the Preparation 01 sleep and wear intervals, and the fixed time-of-day profiles and relevance maps learned in Preparation 03. The main outputs are eight R data files containing participant-day, participant, 30-minute, and hourly values for the near-eye and chest placements. Preparation 06 combines those files with site and participant context; this report does not inspect any hypothesis result.

ImportantInterpret the full-day metrics as a hybrid measurement

During wake and the three hours before sleep, the record represents the logger’s worn placement: near-eye or chest. During diary-defined sleep, it represents the bedside sleep environment. A metric spanning the full day is therefore an environmental-light summary of this planned hybrid record, not a continuous 24-hour measure of ocular exposure.

Exact inputs

Preparation 02 had already retained only participant-days with valid melEDI for at least 80% of the fixed 1,440-minute hybrid day. The 50% requirement within an hour governs whether an hourly value can be calculated; it does not remove otherwise valid one-minute measurements from daily or minute-based metrics. All-finite, all-zero melEDI days had also been removed from the primary coverage files while remaining available as a predefined sensitivity.

NoteShort glossary
  • Metric support: the observed data available at the times relevant to a particular metric; insufficient support makes that metric unavailable but does not remove the participant-day.
  • M10 and L10: the mean melEDI during, respectively, the brightest and darkest 10-hour windows of a participant-day.
  • MDER: the melanopic daylight efficacy ratio, calculated here as the arithmetic mean of viable one-minute melEDI-to-illuminance ratios.
  • Transformed and back-transformed quantities: calculations may be made on a scale chosen for statistical or numerical reasons; back-transformation returns the result to its original practical unit, such as lx.

Scientific rules applied during production

Source-verified zeros after geometric means

Geometric means can include true zero measurements because production first adds 0.1 lx, averages on the log10 scale, transforms back, and subtracts the same 0.1 lx. In exact arithmetic, an all-zero source window returns zero. Floating-point arithmetic can instead leave a minute positive remainder: the eight affected L10 means were 4.163336342344337e-17 lx, far below the source-verified numerical tolerance of 2.220446049250313e-14 lx.

The active numerical-zero rule stores a positive remainder within that tolerance as exact zero only when every finite source minute is itself exactly zero. Missing minutes remain missing; they are counted separately and never imputed as zeros. A materially negative result stops production, and a small positive result with any positive source minute is retained.

Exactly eight primary L10 mean cells changed: three near-eye and five chest. Seven selected 600-minute windows contained 600 observed zero minutes. The remaining chest window contained 547 observed zero minutes and 53 missing minutes; all 547 observed values were zero, and the 53 missing minutes stayed missing. L10 remains available for all 816 near-eye and 902 chest participant-days, so neither the sample nor its support rule changed. Every non-L10 value and all samples are unchanged. No hypothesis model is run or rebuilt by this report.

The sealed audit contains 12 numerical-zero normalizations in total: four previously documented negative roundoff residuals and the eight positive residuals above. A zero-capable analysis retains these participant-days as exact zeros; a later two-part model retains them in its zero/occurrence part and excludes them only from a strictly positive magnitude part by definition.

The central distinction is between participant-day eligibility and metric-specific availability. A day that passed the general coverage rule remains in the prepared data even when, for example, its pre-sleep period is unknown, its M10 window has insufficient support, or its paired illuminance data cannot support MDER.

Cohort and time-grid outputs

Near-eye metrics contain 141 participants from nine sites and 816 participant-days. Chest metrics contain 154 participants from eight sites and 902 participant-days. These are the current stored Preparation 04 samples, not downstream model-specific complete-case samples.

Diary-period support

Wake, the three hours before sleep, and sleep each use a metric-specific 80% support rule. The 70% and 90% columns show the fixed sensitivity cutoffs; they do not change general participant-day eligibility.

At the primary cutoff, the near-eye output retains 737 wake, 655 pre-sleep, and 778 sleep-environment values. The chest output retains 818, 743, and 861, respectively. An unavailable value carries one of three distinct explanations: the diary could not bound the day’s state domains, the target period did not occur, or the target period contained too little valid melEDI.

MDER from viable one-minute channel pairs

The melanopic daylight efficacy ratio (MDER) describes melEDI relative to photopic illuminance. For each participant-day, production first represents the complete 1,440-position local wall-clock grid. If the autumn clock change creates two observations for one local minute, melEDI and illuminance are each averaged within that clock minute before their ratio is formed. A local minute that does not exist during the spring clock change remains missing.

A minute contributes only when both channels are finite and strictly greater than zero. This restriction defines a viable proportional comparison: a zero illuminance denominator is undefined, and a zero in either channel does not represent a meaningful positive channel ratio for this estimand. MDER is the arithmetic mean of the viable one-minute melEDI/illuminance ratios. At least 720 of the 1,440 positions must be viable; the boundary is inclusive, so exactly 720 passes. If fewer are viable, only MDER is unavailable. The participant-day and all other metrics remain eligible.

No time profile weights, gates, or scales enter current MDER, and it is not a ratio of daily channel integrals. Records describing a profile-supported ratio-of-integrals method and 70%, 80%, or 90% MDER gates document an earlier method; they are not active.

The gap-timing-unaware dataset still passed the general 50%-per-hour and 80%-per-day coverage rules. Its name means that the timing of remaining missing observations was not used for an additional metric-specific adjustment. For this first explanation only, the primary dataset could be interpreted as a time-sensitive primary metric dataset; hereafter it is called simply the primary dataset. Both datasets now use the same momentary-ratio MDER definition.

In the primary dataset, MDER is available for 702 of 816 near-eye participant-days from 137 participants (mean 0.7239; median 0.7239) and 732 of 902 chest participant-days from 152 participants (mean 0.7565; median 0.7494). In the gap-timing-unaware dataset, it is available for 687 of 811 near-eye participant-days from 137 participants (mean 0.7243; median 0.7239) and 723 of 897 chest participant-days from 152 participants (mean 0.7567; median 0.7495).

The stored comparison found all 25,620 non-MDER participant-day cells exactly unchanged. A failed MDER support rule therefore removes only MDER, not the day or any other metric.

Thirty-minute and hourly values

The regular grids preserve unsupported periods as explicit missing values. This matters because deleting rows would compress time and could create false adjacency, change a window’s apparent duration, or hide where coverage was lost.

Which metric values are available?

A known absence and an unknown value remain different. For example, an adequately observed day with no exposure above a threshold has a duration of zero, but first, last, and mean timing do not exist. For the longest period above 250 lx, the observed duration remains a lower bound when a missing interval could conceal a longer period; the separate exact-only sensitivity is available only when the lower and possible upper bounds agree.

Gaps and daylight-saving changes

Actual-time metrics retain both occurrences of a repeated autumn hour and respect the true elapsed gap during the spring transition. Clock-aligned metrics use a fixed 24-hour local-clock grid; repeated autumn values are averaged only on that grid, and structurally absent spring minutes remain absent. Missing light, unknown diary state, and a daylight-saving transition are therefore not treated as the same event.

Limitation of current independent verification

Independent reconstruction of current diary-period support remains incomplete.

This qualification applies to the current diary-period support files. It does not apply to current MDER and is not evidence that the diary-period data or any downstream result is wrong. The file checks below do not attempt that scientific reconstruction.

check_manifest <- function(path, family, expected_sha256) {
  manifest_path <- absolute_path(path)
  manifest <- read_stored_csv(path)
  assert_columns(
    manifest,
    c("path", "sha256", "bytes", "producer", "artifact_type"),
    paste(family, "manifest")
  )

  manifest_sha256 <- digest::digest(
    file = manifest_path,
    algo = "sha256"
  )
  stopifnot(identical(manifest_sha256, expected_sha256))

  listed_paths <- absolute_path(manifest$path)
  present <- file.exists(listed_paths)
  current_bytes <- rep(NA_real_, length(listed_paths))
  current_sha256 <- rep(NA_character_, length(listed_paths))
  current_bytes[present] <- file.info(listed_paths[present])$size
  current_sha256[present] <- vapply(
    listed_paths[present],
    function(file) digest::digest(file = file, algo = "sha256"),
    character(1)
  )

  summary <- tibble::tibble(
    Family = family,
    `Manifest path` = path,
    `Manifest SHA-256` = manifest_sha256,
    `Files listed` = nrow(manifest),
    `Files present` = sum(present),
    `Size checks` = sum(present & current_bytes == manifest$bytes),
    `SHA-256 checks` = sum(present & current_sha256 == manifest$sha256),
    Check = ifelse(
      all(
        present &
          current_bytes == manifest$bytes &
          current_sha256 == manifest$sha256
      ),
      "PASS",
      "FAIL"
    )
  )
  stopifnot(summary$Check == "PASS")

  list(data = manifest, summary = summary)
}

## Purpose and position in the preparation chain

Preparation 04 converts the eligible one-minute light records from
Preparation 02 into the daily, diary-period, 30-minute, hourly, and
participant-level metrics used by later analyses. It also records whether
each individual metric had enough relevant data to be interpreted. A failed
support check makes only that metric unavailable; it does not discard the
participant-day from unrelated analyses. For offset geometric means, it also
records when floating-point roundoff is normalized to a source-verified exact
zero.

The exact inputs are the Preparation 02 Rule A coverage files, the
Preparation 01 sleep and wear intervals, and the fixed time-of-day profiles
and relevance maps learned in Preparation 03. The main outputs are eight R
data files containing participant-day, participant, 30-minute, and hourly
values for the near-eye and chest placements. [Preparation 06](06_model_ready_datasets.qmd)
combines those files with site and participant context; this report does not
inspect any hypothesis result.

### Source-verified zeros after geometric means

Geometric means can include true zero measurements because production first
adds 0.1 lx, averages on the log10 scale, transforms back, and subtracts the
same 0.1 lx. In exact arithmetic, an all-zero source window returns zero.
Floating-point arithmetic can instead leave a minute positive remainder: the
eight affected L10 means were
`4.163336342344337e-17` lx, far below the source-verified numerical tolerance
of `2.220446049250313e-14` lx.

The active numerical-zero rule stores a positive remainder within that
tolerance as exact zero only when every finite source minute is itself exactly
zero. Missing minutes remain missing; they are counted separately and never
imputed as zeros. A materially negative result stops production, and a small
positive result with any positive source minute is retained.

### MDER from viable one-minute channel pairs

The melanopic daylight efficacy ratio (MDER) describes melEDI relative to
photopic illuminance. For each participant-day, production first represents
the complete 1,440-position local wall-clock grid. If the autumn clock change
creates two observations for one local minute, melEDI and illuminance are each
averaged within that clock minute before their ratio is formed. A local minute
that does not exist during the spring clock change remains missing.

A minute contributes only when both channels are finite and strictly greater
than zero. This restriction defines a viable proportional comparison: a zero
illuminance denominator is undefined, and a zero in either channel does not
represent a meaningful positive channel ratio for this estimand. MDER is the
arithmetic mean of the viable one-minute melEDI/illuminance ratios. At least
720 of the 1,440 positions must be viable; the boundary is inclusive, so
exactly 720 passes. If fewer are viable, only MDER is unavailable. The
participant-day and all other metrics remain eligible.

No time profile weights, gates, or scales enter current MDER, and it is not a
ratio of daily channel integrals. Records describing a profile-supported
ratio-of-integrals method and 70%, 80%, or 90% MDER gates document an earlier
method; they are not active.

The **gap-timing-unaware dataset** still passed the general 50%-per-hour and
80%-per-day coverage rules. Its name means that the timing of remaining
missing observations was not used for an additional metric-specific
adjustment. For this first explanation only, the primary dataset could be
interpreted as a **time-sensitive primary metric dataset**; hereafter it is
called simply the **primary dataset**. Both datasets now use the same
momentary-ratio MDER definition.
