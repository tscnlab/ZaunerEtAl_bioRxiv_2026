Preparation 03: Learn fixed time-of-day reference profiles

From eligible one-minute records to metric-specific temporal-support maps

Last modified:

August 13, 2026

Purpose and position in the preparation chain

Preparation 03 learned fixed time-of-day reference profiles from the participant-days retained in Preparation 02. These profiles describe when melEDI and illuminance were typically observed across the day while giving each participant equal influence. Preparation 04 uses them to judge whether a participant-day was observed at the times that matter for a particular metric. The profiles do not replace an observed measurement and, except for the explicitly permitted additive dose correction, do not change a metric value.

The exact scientific inputs were:

  • near-eye Rule A one-minute records;
  • chest Rule A one-minute records;
  • the stored Rule A settings.

The outputs are the fixed intensity profiles, the strict-threshold timing distribution, metric-specific relevance maps, their support summaries, and the provenance records under artifacts/04_reference_profiles/. They are passed to Preparation 04.

How the reference profiles are defined

The profiles separate time alignment, participant weighting, and support requirements so that each scientific decision is explicit. Table 1 states the rules in the order in which they affected the data.

The complete full day is the intended hybrid 24-hour measurement cycle, including bedside measurements during diary-defined sleep. Wake, sleep, and the three hours before sleep are separate profile domains; they do not redefine which days passed the general coverage rule.

What the pooled profiles look like

Figure 2 shows the pooled full-day profiles that provide the clearest overview of the learned time-of-day pattern. Each point is one stored 30-minute reference value. The figure is descriptive: its lines connect stored values and do not interpolate missing support.

Download the source data for Figure 2.

Exact inputs and clock handling

The near-eye and chest input files retained their actual-time rows and were identified before profile learning. Table 3 shows the participant and minute support for each signal. The slight signal-specific differences in usable minutes reflect the stored missing values; one signal was not used to erase an observed value in the other.

Autumn daylight-saving changes can assign the same local clock label to two different actual observations. Preparation 03 averaged those repeated labels only in the separate local-clock copy used for learning. It kept both observations in the fixed actual-time input. For diary-period profiles, the period was selected in actual time before repeated local labels were averaged, so a repeated clock label could not move an observation across a sleep or wake boundary.

Support for the intensity profiles

Each profile retains all 48 half-hour periods. A period without the required number of participants remains in the stored table with no usable reference value; it is never interpolated. Consequently, a profile can be estimable overall while containing explicitly unsupported periods. Table 5 summarizes all 288 stored profile groups.

Full-day and pooled wake profiles had support in every stored half-hour period. Support was sparser within sleep and the three hours before sleep, especially in individual-site profiles. Five site-specific sleep profiles were not estimable; the stored diagnostics preserve that limitation rather than supplying a replacement.

Probability of exceeding 250 lx by time of day

The timing of strict melEDI values above 250 lx cannot be inferred from whether the population median profile itself exceeds 250 lx. Individual participant-days may cross the threshold even when the population median remains lower. Preparation 03 therefore learned a separate probability for each half-hour period:

  1. within every participant-day, it calculated the fraction of valid minutes with melEDI strictly greater than 250 lx;
  2. it gave days equal weight within each participant; and
  3. it then gave participants equal weight within the pooled, site-specific, or leave-one-site-out distribution.

Table 6 shows that all 36 stored distributions were estimable and had positive probability in at least some periods. Preparation 04 expands each half-hour probability across its 30 constituent minutes only to measure temporal support. It does not weight or change an observed timing.

How Preparation 04 may use each map

The intensity and threshold distributions were converted into metric-specific relevance maps. These maps state which times of day matter for checking the observation support of a metric. They do not all have permission to change a value. Table 7 makes that distinction explicit.

The M10 and L10 maps check whether enough relevant times were observed for the brightest and darkest 10-hour windows. The timing map checks support for the first, last, and mean timing above 250 lx. None of these active support-only maps scales a level, duration, or timing, and no L5 profile or map was produced.

For current MDER, the daily value is the arithmetic mean of viable one-minute melEDI-to-illuminance ratios. No reference profile weights, scales, or gates that calculation. The stored paired-channel maps describe an earlier ratio-of-integrals method and are retained only as historical provenance; they are not active Preparation 04 inputs for MDER.

Limitation of current independent verification

Independent reconstruction of the current reference-profile input remains incomplete.

The current files pass the identity and internal-consistency checks described below, but those checks do not independently reconstruct the scientific values. This incomplete provenance check is not evidence that a current profile, metric, or downstream result is incorrect.

Handoff to Preparation 04

Preparation 03 passes forward fixed, versioned temporal reference information: 13,824 intensity-profile rows, 1,728 strict-threshold distribution rows, and 36,288 metric-relevance rows, together with their support diagnostics, settings, input fingerprints, and artifact manifest. Preparation 04 may read and apply those stored objects under their recorded permissions. It may not relearn, interpolate, or silently substitute a profile during rendering.

read_stored_csv <- function(path) {
  full_path <- absolute_path(path)

assert_columns <- function(data, required, label) {
  missing_columns <- setdiff(required, names(data))
  if (length(missing_columns) > 0L) {
    stop(
      label,
      " is missing required columns: ",
      paste(missing_columns, collapse = ", ")
    )
  }
  invisible(data)
}

prep_gt <- function(data, source_note = NULL, groupname_col = NULL) {
  output <- gt::gt(data, groupname_col = groupname_col) |>
    gt::opt_row_striping() |>
    gt::sub_missing(missing_text = "—") |>
    gt::tab_options(
      table.font.size = gt::px(13),
      table.width = gt::pct(100),
      data_row.padding = gt::px(4),
      heading.align = "left",
      source_notes.font.size = gt::px(11)
    )
  if (!is.null(source_note)) {
    output <- output |>
      gt::tab_source_note(gt::md(source_note))
  }
  output
}

## Purpose and position in the preparation chain

Preparation 03 learned fixed time-of-day reference profiles from the
participant-days retained in [Preparation 02](02_coverage_sample_flow.qmd).
These profiles describe when melEDI and illuminance were typically observed
across the day while giving each participant equal influence. Preparation 04
uses them to judge whether a participant-day was observed at the times that
matter for a particular metric. The profiles do not replace an observed
measurement and, except for the explicitly permitted additive dose
correction, do not change a metric value.

The exact scientific inputs were:

- [near-eye Rule A one-minute records](../../artifacts/03_coverage/light_glasses_coverage.rds);
- [chest Rule A one-minute records](../../artifacts/03_coverage/light_chest_coverage.rds);
- [the stored Rule A settings](../../artifacts/03_coverage/coverage_settings.csv).

The outputs are the fixed intensity profiles, the strict-threshold timing
distribution, metric-specific relevance maps, their support summaries, and
the provenance records under
`artifacts/04_reference_profiles/`. They are passed to
[Preparation 04](04_metric_derivation.qmd).

## Probability of exceeding 250 lx by time of day

The timing of strict melEDI values above 250 lx cannot be inferred from whether
the population median profile itself exceeds 250 lx. Individual
participant-days may cross the threshold even when the population median
remains lower. Preparation 03 therefore learned a separate probability for
each half-hour period:

1. within every participant-day, it calculated the fraction of valid minutes
   with melEDI strictly greater than 250 lx;
2. it gave days equal weight within each participant; and
3. it then gave participants equal weight within the pooled, site-specific,
   or leave-one-site-out distribution.

@tbl-threshold-timing-support shows that all 36 stored distributions were
estimable and had positive probability in at least some periods. Preparation
04 expands each half-hour probability across its 30 constituent minutes only
to measure temporal support. It does not weight or change an observed timing.

ratio-of-integrals method and are retained only as historical provenance; they
