From verified one-hour records and reported light sources to site-average exposure contrasts

Last modified:

August 14, 2026

About this analysis record

H03 asks whether all seven prespecified self-reported primary-light-source categories are associated with measured one-hour personal light exposure. The primary near-eye sensor position measures light close to the eyes. The complementary chest sensor position measures light at the chest and is not a measure of ocular exposure. The outcome is melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI). The central estimand is the site-average geometric expected melEDI for each category and its ratio to indoor electric light. A site-average estimate gives each included site equal weight on the fitted log-mean scale; one back-transformation then returns the estimate to melEDI in lux.

This page identifies the data and code that produced the result, explains why each analytical operation was needed, and shows the recorded evidence used to check it. The analysis is observational and does not identify causal effects, health outcomes, or equivalence between measurement placements.

The H03 results report presents the scientific findings and its linked registration-record summary. The metric and model-ready input definitions are also documented in Preparation 04 and Preparation 06.

NoteScope of this analysis record

This page reads accepted estimates, 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs), omnibus tests, model checks, influence refits, sensitivity results, exploratory smooths, and latitude results from their recorded files. Its bounded display work does not fit or refit a model, calculate predictions, re-estimate residual autocorrelation, rerun a leave-one-site-out analysis, bootstrap, simulate data, or recalculate a variance allocation.

Schema, keys, missingness, and category overlap

Outcome and diary records were joined one-to-one by site, participant, local date, and local clock hour. The primary model frames contain only admissible outcome hours with a non-missing primary-light-source category. Each retained record has one analysis category. The normalized diary also retains seven source-indicator flags: 7,109 near-eye hours and 8,012 chest hours had more than one true source flag, but the declared primary category was one of those true flags and remained the single mutually exclusive analysis label. Thus the overlap information was audited without duplicating an hour across categories.

3. Population-mean and category-by-site interaction models

Additive population-mean model

The prespecified primary question is tested with a population-mean GLM:

geo_medi_1h ~ site + light_source

geo_medi_1h is the one-hour zero-aware geometric melEDI in lux. The model uses a log link and a quasi-Tweedie variance function Var(Y | X) = phi * mu^1.539919. Site is a fixed factor with sum contrasts; light source is a fixed seven-level factor with indoor electric light as the treatment reference. The formula contains no random effect. Repeated participant-hours are addressed for inference with participant-clustered HC1 sandwich covariance, finite denominator degrees of freedom equal to the number of participant clusters minus one, and two-sided 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs). This interval calculation accounts for the fact that observations from the same participant may be related.

Cluster-robust covariance protects coefficient uncertainty against unspecified within-participant dependence under its large-cluster assumptions; it does not remove serial structure from residuals or change the fitted conditional mean. Participant-day repetition and within-day temporal dependence are therefore also examined directly in model checks and sensitivity analyses.

6. Exploratory nonlinear time-of-day and linear-latitude context

Nonlinear time-of-day GAM analysis

The nonlinear generalized additive model (GAM) analysis is exploratory and separate from the preregistered GLM inference. The association with clock time is allowed to bend across the day rather than follow a straight line. It models the raw one-hour geometric melEDI outcome with a fixed-power Tweedie mean, log link, global cyclic time smooth, sum-to-zero light-source and site deviation smooths, participant-specific smooths, and participant-day random intercepts:

geo_medi_1h ~
  s(time_hour, bs = "cc", k = 12) +
  s(time_hour, light_source, bs = "sz", k = 12) +
  s(time_hour, site, bs = "sz", k = 12) +
  s(time_hour, participant, bs = "fs", k = 10) +
  s(participant_day, bs = "re")

The sum-to-zero smooths add category- and site-specific deviations to the global cyclic time-of-day smooth; indoor electric light is not a smooth reference. bam() used fREML, discrete computation, cyclic knots at 0 and 24 hours, and a second fit with an AR(1) working error parameter estimated from the preliminary standardized residual lag-1 correlation. ## About this analysis record

H03 asks whether all seven prespecified self-reported primary-light-source
categories are associated with measured one-hour personal light exposure.
The primary near-eye sensor position measures light close to the eyes. The
complementary chest sensor position measures light at the chest and is not a
measure of ocular exposure. The outcome is melanopic equivalent daylight
illuminance (melEDI). The central estimand is the site-average geometric
expected melEDI for each category and its ratio to indoor electric light. A
site-average estimate gives each included site equal weight on the fitted
log-mean scale; one back-transformation then returns the estimate to melEDI in
lux.

This page identifies the data and code that produced the result, explains why
each analytical operation was needed, and shows the recorded evidence used to
check it. The analysis is observational and does not identify causal effects,
health outcomes, or equivalence between measurement placements.

The [H03 results report](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H03.qmd) presents the
scientific findings and its [linked registration-record summary](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H03.qmd#h03-preregistration-deviations).
The metric and model-ready input definitions are also
documented in [Preparation 04](../../../notebooks/preparation/04_metric_derivation.qmd)
and [Preparation 06](../../../notebooks/preparation/06_model_ready_datasets.qmd).

::: {.callout-note title="Scope of this analysis record"}

omnibus tests, model checks, influence refits, sensitivity results,
exploratory smooths, and latitude results from their recorded files. Its

## 3. Population-mean and category-by-site interaction models

### Additive population-mean model

The prespecified primary question is tested with a population-mean GLM:

```r
geo_medi_1h ~ site + light_source
```

`geo_medi_1h` is the one-hour zero-aware geometric melEDI in lux. The model
uses a log link and a quasi-Tweedie variance function
`Var(Y | X) = phi * mu^1.539919`. Site is a fixed factor with sum contrasts;
light source is a fixed seven-level factor with indoor electric light as the
treatment reference. The formula contains no random effect. Repeated
participant-hours are addressed for inference with participant-clustered HC1
sandwich covariance, finite denominator degrees of freedom equal to the
number of participant clusters minus one, and two-sided 95% confidence
intervals (95% CIs). This interval calculation accounts for the fact that
observations from the same participant may be related.

Cluster-robust covariance protects coefficient uncertainty against unspecified
within-participant dependence under its large-cluster assumptions; it does
not remove serial structure from residuals or change the fitted conditional
mean. Participant-day repetition and within-day temporal dependence are
therefore also examined directly in model checks and sensitivity analyses.

### Omnibus test, category contrasts, and site-average estimates

non-reference light-source coefficients equal zero. The near-eye test is the
preregistered primary inference; the chest test is complementary. The six

### Category-by-site interaction

The association between light-source category and melEDI is allowed to differ
by study site in a model examined separately from the additive primary
omnibus. The near-eye frame supports the literal interaction:

```r
geo_medi_1h ~ site * light_source
```

The chest frame lacks all categories at the near-eye-only Munich (DE) site and is
therefore represented by one coefficient for each observed site-category
cell:

```r
geo_medi_1h ~ 0 + site_source_cell
```

The second expression is an equivalent saturated observed-cell
parameterization, not a different scientific estimand. Its restriction matrix
tests whether the observed cells can be represented additively by site plus
light source. The models use the same log link, quasi-Tweedie variance,
participant-clustered HC1 covariance, and geometric site-average estimation as
the additive models.

## 6. Exploratory nonlinear time-of-day and linear-latitude context

### Nonlinear time-of-day GAM analysis

The nonlinear generalized additive model (GAM) analysis is exploratory and
separate from the preregistered GLM inference. The association with clock time
is allowed to bend across the day rather than follow a straight line. It
models the raw one-hour geometric melEDI outcome with a fixed-power Tweedie
mean, log link, global cyclic time smooth, sum-to-zero light-source and site
deviation smooths, participant-specific smooths, and participant-day random
intercepts:

```r
geo_medi_1h ~
  s(time_hour, bs = "cc", k = 12) +
  s(time_hour, light_source, bs = "sz", k = 12) +
  s(time_hour, site, bs = "sz", k = 12) +
  s(time_hour, participant, bs = "fs", k = 10) +
  s(participant_day, bs = "re")
```

The sum-to-zero smooths add category- and site-specific deviations to the
global cyclic time-of-day smooth; indoor electric light is not a smooth
reference. `bam()` used fREML, discrete computation, cyclic knots at 0 and 24
hours, and a second fit with an AR(1) working error parameter estimated from
the preliminary standardized residual lag-1 correlation. Participant,
participant-day, and site smooths remain in the fitted model but are excluded
from the displayed population curves. The stored figure curves form a
site-average by giving sites equal weight and then omit unsupported time
points from the display.

### Linear absolute-latitude context

The exploratory ecological replacement of site uses centered absolute
latitude and its category interaction:

```r
geo_medi_1h ~ light_source * absolute_latitude_centered_10deg
```

It uses the same quasi-Tweedie working power and log link, but uncertainty is
clustered by site because latitude varies only between sites. Seven
category-specific slopes form one FDR family within placement. Leave-one-site-
out fits check direction and point-estimate range; they do not create a
larger effective site sample.
