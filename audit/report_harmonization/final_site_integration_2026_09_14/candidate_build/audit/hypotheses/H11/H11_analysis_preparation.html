From verified H02 temporal frames and recorded biological sex to complete-curve inference

Last modified:

August 12, 2026

Purpose

This page explains how the verified H02 temporal frames and recorded biological sex were turned into the inference presented in the H11 results report.

H11 asks whether the 24-hour pattern of personal melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI) differs by the biological-sex variable recorded in the study. Biological sex and gender identity are distinct constructs; gender identity was not analysed. The near-eye sensor position is primary. The chest sensor position provides complementary evidence and is not a measure of ocular exposure. This placement change is recorded in DEV-001.

The reported estimand is the complete Female-minus-Male fitted curve across 48 half-hour clock bins, averaged equally across included sites and conditional on the fitted temporal, participant, participant-day, site, and autocorrelation structure. A site-average fitted mean averages across included sites and gives each site equal weight. The participant is the independent unit for robust inference. A participant-day is one participant’s included observations on one local calendar day; model rows are 30-minute observations nested within participant-days and participants.

The accepted preregistration differences are consolidated in the H11 preregistration-deviations section.

Temporal and analytical-frame construction

Inherited time structure

H11 inherits the finalized temporal architecture documented in the H02 analysis preparation and provenance rather than selecting a new one. Local wall-clock time is represented by 48 half-hour midpoints from 00:15 to 23:45. The smooth is cyclic, meaning that it joins smoothly across midnight. Site-specific time patterns sum to zero, participant-specific time curves represent remaining within-participant variation, and participant-day random effects represent remaining variation within local calendar days. The hierarchy and site structure are recorded in DEV-044 and DEV-045.

Autocorrelation sequences are based on true elapsed instants. A sequence restarts at participant-day boundaries, elapsed-time discontinuities, and non-one-to-one wall-clock outcomes, including daylight-saving-time ambiguity. The accepted frames therefore do not infer continuity merely because two rows have adjacent clock labels.

2. Nonlinear GAM construction

A nonlinear generalized additive model (GAM) allows the daily melEDI pattern to vary smoothly and nonlinearly over clock time. Cyclic smooths join smoothly across midnight. Participant-specific time curves and participant-day random effects represent remaining repeated-measure variation after the shared time, biological-sex, and site structure is considered. Autocorrelation is residual similarity between observations close together in time; an AR(1) structure represents this similarity as stronger at shorter lags.

3. Estimand, uncertainty, and multiplicity

Complete-curve inference

The complete-curve, or global, test is one joint test of the time-constant and time-varying Female-minus-Male contributions across the whole 48-bin, 24-hour curve within each placement and dataset. Inference uses participant-summed, AR-whitened scores; a participant-cluster CR1 covariance; the smoothing-bias term V_p-V_e; and a finite-cluster, fractional-rank F reference. A pointwise 95% confidence interval (95% CI) applies to one displayed clock bin. It is not a simultaneous band across the full day. All displayed curve, contrast, and scalar intervals are pointwise 95% CIs.

The primary near-eye global raw p-value is 0.028 and the complementary chest raw p-value is 0.029. Each is the sole member of its explicitly labelled global family, so its stored adjusted value is numerically identical. FDR adjustment is applied within separately labelled families. This complete-curve inference and multiplicity specification is recorded in DEV-048.

What the two-component decomposition can and cannot say

The complete-curve result is the overall test. A separate, secondary decomposition asks whether that curve difference can be attributed cleanly to (1) a constant parametric level component or (2) a cyclic shape component. Those two attribution tests form a two-test FDR family within each placement and dataset.

No component remains supported after that two-test adjustment. This does not invalidate or reverse the significant global curve result. It means only that the evidence cannot cleanly separate the overall curve association into an independent “level” contribution and an independent “shape” contribution after accounting for the two attribution attempts. The two components are parameterization-dependent parts of one fitted curve and are not a second omnibus test of whether the full curve differs.

Conditional effect-size branch

The predeclared effect-size branch opens only when the placement-specific raw global test has p<0.050. It opened for both primary placements. The reported curve-variation estimator is the mean, across 48 clock bins, of the sample variance across the two equally weighted fitted biological-sex curves. It is a conditional descriptive magnitude on the squared model-prediction scale, not variance explained. Its 95% interval uses the accepted participant-cluster robust covariance and first-order delta method.

5. Sensitivity analyses and deferred comparisons

Gap timing

The gap-timing-unaware dataset still passed the general requirement for at least 50% valid data per hour and at least 80% valid data per participant-day. “Gap-timing-unaware” means that the timing of the remaining missing observations is not used for metric-specific adjustment. For contrast only in this first explanation, the primary dataset could be interpreted as a time-sensitive primary metric dataset; hereafter it is called simply the primary dataset.

Applying the same model and global test to the gap-timing-unaware dataset gave raw global p-values of 0.026 near eye and 0.037 at chest. This supports stability to whether the timing of remaining missing observations is used for metric-specific adjustment. It does not test every possible missing-data mechanism.

This page explains how the verified H02 temporal frames and recorded biological
sex were turned into the inference presented in the
[H11 results report](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H11.qmd).

H11 asks whether the 24-hour pattern of personal melanopic equivalent daylight
illuminance (melEDI) differs by the biological-sex variable recorded in the
study. Biological sex and gender identity are distinct constructs; gender
identity was not analysed. The near-eye sensor position is primary. The chest
sensor position provides complementary evidence and is not a measure of ocular
exposure. This placement change is recorded in
[`DEV-001`](../../../notebooks/preregistration_deviations.qmd#dev-001).

The reported estimand is the complete Female-minus-Male fitted curve across 48
half-hour clock bins, averaged equally across included sites and conditional on
the fitted temporal, participant, participant-day, site, and autocorrelation
structure. A site-average fitted mean averages across included sites and gives
each site equal weight. The participant is the independent unit for robust
inference. A participant-day is one participant's included observations on one
local calendar day; model rows are 30-minute observations nested within
participant-days and participants.

The accepted preregistration differences are consolidated in the
[H11 preregistration-deviations section](../../../notebooks/hypotheses/H11.qmd#h11-preregistration-deviations).

## 2. Nonlinear GAM construction

A nonlinear generalized additive model (GAM) allows the daily melEDI pattern
to vary smoothly and nonlinearly over clock time. Cyclic smooths join smoothly
across midnight. Participant-specific time curves and participant-day random
effects represent remaining repeated-measure variation after the shared time,
biological-sex, and site structure is considered. Autocorrelation is residual
similarity between observations close together in time; an AR(1) structure
represents this similarity as stronger at shorter lags.

The sex factor uses Male as the treatment-reference level. The ordered
`sex_smooth` factor identifies the cyclic Female-minus-Male deviation. The
main cyclic smooth is the Male reference curve; the sex-deviation smooth adds
the Female shape difference. Site deviations use the sum-to-zero basis,
participant curves use factor smooths, and participant-day random intercepts
account for repeated observations within days. In the activity model, Home is
the treatment reference and the ordered activity deviation smooths share one
smoothing parameter (`id = 2`).

## 3. Estimand, uncertainty, and multiplicity

### Complete-curve inference

The complete-curve, or global, test is one joint test of the time-constant and
time-varying Female-minus-Male contributions across the whole 48-bin, 24-hour
curve within each placement and dataset. Inference
uses participant-summed, AR-whitened scores; a participant-cluster CR1
covariance; the smoothing-bias term $V_p-V_e$; and a finite-cluster,
fractional-rank $F$ reference. A pointwise 95% confidence interval (95% CI)
applies to one displayed clock bin. It is not a simultaneous band across the
full day. All displayed curve, contrast, and scalar intervals are pointwise 95%
CIs.

The primary near-eye global raw p-value is
`r format_p(near_global$p_raw)` and the complementary chest raw p-value is
`r format_p(chest_global$p_raw)`. Each is the sole member of its explicitly
labelled global family, so its stored adjusted value is numerically identical.
FDR adjustment is applied within separately labelled families. This
complete-curve inference and multiplicity specification is
recorded in
[`DEV-048`](../../../notebooks/preregistration_deviations.qmd#dev-048).


### What the two-component decomposition can and cannot say

The complete-curve result is the overall test. A separate, secondary
decomposition asks whether that curve difference can be attributed cleanly to
(1) a constant parametric level component or (2) a cyclic shape component.
Those two attribution tests form a two-test FDR family within each placement
and dataset.

No component remains supported after that two-test adjustment. This does
**not** invalidate or reverse the significant global curve result. It means
only that the evidence cannot cleanly separate the overall curve association
into an independent “level” contribution and an independent “shape”
contribution after accounting for the two attribution attempts. The two
components are parameterization-dependent parts of one fitted curve and are
not a second omnibus test of whether the full curve differs.


### Conditional effect-size branch

The predeclared effect-size branch opens only when the placement-specific raw
global test has $p<0.050$. It opened for both primary placements. The reported
curve-variation estimator is the mean, across 48 clock bins, of the sample
variance across the two equally weighted fitted biological-sex curves. It is a
conditional descriptive magnitude on the squared model-prediction scale, not
variance explained. Its 95% interval uses the accepted participant-cluster
robust covariance and first-order delta method.
