From verified Visual Light Sensitivity Questionnaire (VLSQ-8) scores and personal light-exposure metrics to reported associations

Last modified:

August 12, 2026

NoteAbout this analysis record

This page explains how verified Visual Light Sensitivity Questionnaire (VLSQ-8) scores and personal light-exposure metrics were turned into the H08 results. Higher VLSQ-8 scores indicate greater self-reported visual light sensitivity according to the accepted scale. The companion H08 results report presents the scientific findings.

Purpose

H08 asks whether nine duration-, exposure-history-, and level-based personal light-exposure metrics are associated with VLSQ-8 visual-light-sensitivity scores. The primary near-eye sensor position more closely represents light near the eyes during wear. The complementary chest sensor position is not an ocular-exposure measure and is not pooled with near-eye measurement. Melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI) expresses light in terms of melanopsin-weighted visual-system sensitivity. Every inferential outcome has one row per participant-day, defined as one participant’s eligible local calendar day.

Reported effects compare VLSQ-8 scores separated by one participant-level standard deviation (SD), equal to 5.540 points. Time below 10 lx melEDI before sleep is reported as an absolute difference in hours; the other eight log-link outcomes use ratios and corresponding percentage changes. A back-transformed estimate returns a fitted coefficient to its stated hour difference, ratio, or percentage-change scale. A 95% confidence interval (95% CI) describes uncertainty; Wald construction is named where relevant.

The site-average association averages across sites with equal weight. The separate VLSQ-8-by-site interaction allows the association between VLSQ-8 and a light metric to differ by site. False-discovery-rate (FDR) adjustment is applied within each declared complete nine-metric family.

The predefined preparation sensitivity uses the gap-timing-unaware dataset. It still passed the general requirement of at least 50% valid minutes per hour and at least 80% valid hours per day, but the timing of the remaining missing observations was not used for an additional metric-specific adjustment. For contrast at this first explanation, the primary could be interpreted as a time-sensitive primary metric dataset. Hereafter it is called simply the primary dataset.

This page identifies the exact data, score rule, samples, formulae, code, and stored checks behind the accepted report. It documents provenance; it does not create an alternate analysis.

2. VLSQ-8 predictor

Scale and score reconstruction

VLSQ-8 contains eight ordered item codes from 1 to 5. For every participant, the stored score equalled the item sum plus 5. The constant offset does not affect a centred slope, but retaining it is necessary to reproduce the approved 13–39 score scale. All 184 participants had complete item and total scores. The score was centred at its observed participant mean, 21.598, and reported effects compare scores separated by the participant SD of 5.540 points. Scores were not standardised within site.

3. Metrics and analytical samples

Metric definitions and missing values

The nine outcomes retain the manuscript names and melEDI units. Unsupported values remain missing; they are never converted to darkness or zero. Four metrics use Gaussian models after log10(value + 0.1) transformation, three non-negative duration metrics use Tweedie models with a log link, and two use Gaussian identity models. Practical effects are ratios except for time below 10 lx melEDI before sleep, which is an hour difference.

Current metric derivations are documented in Preparation 04, and the corresponding model-ready inputs in Preparation 06.

The longest continuous period above 250 lx melEDI can be a lower bound when a remaining gap interrupts the observed period. A separate exact-identification sensitivity retains only participant-days for which both ends of the longest period are observed. The corrected melEDI dose uses the registered time-sensitive value; observed uncorrected dose is retained as a descriptive sensitivity on the same fitted sample.

4. Model construction and inference

Exact formulae supplied to the models

The primary specification uses fixed site with sum-to-zero contrasts and a participant random intercept nested within site. This participant random effect represents remaining between-participant variation after site and VLSQ-8 are considered. The additive coefficient is therefore the site-average VLSQ-8 slope rather than a slope at one reference site. A likelihood-ratio comparison of the site-only and additive models tests the site-average association. Comparing the additive and interaction models tests the VLSQ-8-by-site interaction. ## Purpose

H08 asks whether nine duration-, exposure-history-, and level-based personal
light-exposure metrics are associated with VLSQ-8 visual-light-sensitivity
scores. The primary **near-eye sensor position** more closely represents light
near the eyes during wear. The complementary **chest sensor position** is not
an ocular-exposure measure and is not pooled with near-eye measurement.
**Melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melEDI)** expresses light in
terms of melanopsin-weighted visual-system sensitivity. Every inferential
outcome has one row per **participant-day**, defined as one participant's
eligible local calendar day.

Reported effects compare VLSQ-8 scores separated by one participant-level
**standard deviation (SD)**, equal to 5.540 points. Time below 10 lx melEDI
before sleep is reported as an absolute difference in hours; the other eight
log-link outcomes use ratios and corresponding percentage changes. A
**back-transformed** estimate returns a fitted coefficient to its stated hour
difference, ratio, or percentage-change scale. A **95% confidence interval
(95% CI)** describes uncertainty; Wald construction is named where relevant.

The **site-average association** averages across sites with equal weight. The
separate **VLSQ-8-by-site interaction** allows the association between VLSQ-8
and a light metric to differ by site. **False-discovery-rate (FDR)
adjustment** is applied within each declared complete nine-metric family.

The predefined preparation sensitivity uses the **gap-timing-unaware
dataset**. It still passed the general requirement of at least 50% valid
minutes per hour and at least 80% valid hours per day, but the timing of the
remaining missing observations was not used for an additional metric-specific
adjustment. For contrast at this first explanation, the primary could be
interpreted as a **time-sensitive primary metric dataset**. Hereafter it is
called simply the primary dataset.

This page identifies the exact data, score rule, samples, formulae, code, and
stored checks behind the accepted report. It documents provenance; it does
not create an alternate analysis.

## 4. Model construction and inference

### Exact formulae supplied to the models

The primary specification uses fixed site with sum-to-zero contrasts and a
participant random intercept nested within site. This participant **random
effect** represents remaining between-participant variation after site and
VLSQ-8 are considered. The additive coefficient is therefore the site-average
VLSQ-8 slope rather than a slope at one reference site. A likelihood-ratio
comparison of the site-only and additive models tests the site-average
association. Comparing the additive and interaction models tests the
VLSQ-8-by-site interaction. Photoperiod adjustment and participant-level
summary models are structured sensitivities. This separation is the current
scientific choice recorded as
[`DEV-035`](../../../notebooks/preregistration_deviations.qmd#dev-035).

The table retains the exact evaluated Wilkinson formulae used for the current
