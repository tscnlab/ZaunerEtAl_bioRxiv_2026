## Resolved final Phase 1 gate

### Revised one-sentence thesis and Brown placement

**Proposed final Phase 1 thesis**

Across nine sites in seven countries, harmonized near-eye measurements reveal a shared daily architecture of ocular light exposure, with differences among people and days within sites exceeding differences among sites; only 24.0% of valid waking minutes reached the recommended daytime range, whereas 63.3% of pre-sleep and 87.7% of sleep-environment minutes fell within their respective upper limits.

The author approved both:

1. this revised one-sentence thesis; and
2. prominent main-Results placement of the three Brown recommendation-context fractions with exact valid-minute denominators and no participant-level or health-effect interpretation.

## Journal-fit decisions

### 6. Nature Health engagement requirement

Resolved factually on 2026-08-14: the protocol was first trialled with participants at the Tübingen site, and their feedback informed procedural refinements before implementation at the remaining sites. These participants form the Tübingen cohort in the present analysis and are not a separate pilot sample. The manuscript reports this formative participant input with the protocol citation and does not relabel it as formal participant or community co-design.

Editorial risk is reduced but not eliminated: Nature Health states that it requires meaningful engagement with relevant participants and communities. The documented Tübingen feedback supports the participant-engagement component, but it does not establish formal community partnership or engagement throughout interpretation and dissemination.

### 7. Exposure-only fit

No health outcome was measured. Author decision needed: proceed with Nature Health on the basis of environmental exposure epidemiology and measurement relevance, or retain an alternative journal strategy in parallel. The manuscript can establish a measurement and exposure baseline, not health impact.

### 8. Article versus Resource

Recommendation: target an Article because the central contribution is a scientific explanation of multiscale exposure architecture. Choose Resource only if broad reuse of the dataset should supersede that argument as the main editorial contribution.

### 9. Use of personal light exposome

Recommendation: use personal light exposure in the title and principal claims. If personal light exposome is retained, define it narrowly and acknowledge that social, occupational, meteorological, built-environment, and other exposures were not comprehensively measured.

### 11. Recommendation-context and adherence results

Resolved: fractions of valid measured minutes within recommendation contexts belong in the main descriptive Results and remain distinct from the accepted state-period adherence model. The pooled fractions must not be called participant-level adherence, compliance, adequacy or biological response. The modelled state-period proportions may be called adherence, with their endpoint, denominator, coverage, multiplicity and temporal-dependence qualifications.

### 12. Hourly main and daily complement

The scientific role is fixed: the hourly analysis is main and the daily-metric analysis is complementary. Author decision remains on presentation prominence. Recommendation: include one concise complementary paragraph in the main Results because the different daily estimand changes the interpretation of day type and previous sleep, and place the complete daily matrix in Supplementary Information.

### 13. Person-level findings display

The accepted evidence contains two broad null patterns, selective chronotype and age findings, metric-level biological-sex results, and a separate whole-curve sex result. Decide whether a composite main figure should synthesize these roles or whether the main text should show the whole-curve result and leave the other person-level displays supplementary.

The public preprint uses overlapping MeLiDos data and must not be called independent replication. Author decision remains whether this becomes a compact main display, an Extended Data display, or a cross-cutting textual boundary plus Supplementary Information.
