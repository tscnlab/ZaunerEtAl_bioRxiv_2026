<!-- P-R04A -->
The main adherence model included 2,216 recommendation-window periods from 140 participants and 782 complete daytime, pre-sleep and sleep cycles, representing 1,028,958 valid minutes. Site-average work-day adherence was 26.5% during daytime, 61.9% during pre-sleep and 90.3% during sleep; free-day adherence was 21.6%, 69.9% and 84.1%. Relative to work days, free-day adherence was 4.92 percentage points lower during daytime (95% confidence interval (95% CI) 2.21 to 7.64 lower), 7.98 higher during pre-sleep (3.28 to 12.67) and 6.13 lower during sleep (3.48 to 8.79). Requiring at least 80% valid coverage preserved each direction and interval conclusion, with a maximum 0.88-point change.

<!-- P-R04B -->
Adherence differences between free and work days varied among sites (*p* = 0.003): during daytime (*p* = 0.002) and sleep (*p* = 0.003), but not pre-sleep (*p* = 0.526). The free-minus-work daytime difference exceeded its site-average value by 15.0 percentage points in Dortmund (DE; 95% CI 6.9 to 23.1; *p* = 0.004) and was 9.2 lower in Madrid (ES; -15.6 to -2.8; *p* = 0.042); the sleep difference was 6.3 higher in Kumasi (GH; 3.4 to 9.1; *p* < 0.001). These directions persisted when the sensitivity analysis required at least 80% valid data coverage.

<!-- P-R04C -->
Fixed predictors explained 58.4% of variance on the design-standardized response scale, the participant intercept added 2.2 percentage points and observation and distribution variation accounted for 39.4%; conditional R² was 60.6%. Within marginal R², Shapley shares were 94.1% for recommendation window, 4.8% for site and 1.2% for day type. On the design-standardized response scale, site accounted for 2.8 percentage points of variance, compared with a 2.2-point participant-intercept increment and 0.7 percentage points for day type. Thus, the site allocation was about 1.3 times the participant-intercept increment and about four times the day-type allocation. @tbl-brown-adherence summarises the main estimates; [Supplementary Figures S4](#fig-s4) and [S5](#fig-s5) show site-average and site-specific results.

<!-- P-R04D -->
A separate exploratory analysis related adherence across recommendation windows. Within participants, the estimated differences for a daytime-adherence value 10 percentage points above a participant's usual level were -0.26 percentage points for sleep adherence (95% CI -0.95 to 0.43) and -1.09 points for pre-sleep adherence (-2.75 to 0.56). Both intervals included zero, and temporal dependence remained unresolved, so we withheld a within-participant day-level claim rather than interpreting these estimates as evidence of no association. Between participants, each 10 percentage points higher observed average daytime adherence was associated with 2.52 points lower sleep adherence (95% CI 1.48 to 3.55 lower; FDR-adjusted *p* < 0.001) and 3.59 points lower pre-sleep adherence (1.25 to 5.94 lower; FDR-adjusted *p* = 0.005). Directions and interval conclusions remained in the sensitivity analysis, with a maximum 0.60-point change. Thus, participants with higher average daytime adherence tended to have lower pre-sleep and sleep adherence, consistent with higher evening and nighttime light exposure. Complete estimates are reported in [Supplementary Table S4](#supp-table-s4), with participant profiles in [Supplementary Figure S6](#fig-s6).

<!-- P-R18 -->
A parallel analysis of overlapping MeLiDos data reported structured position differences across scale, metric, context, site, day and participant [@zauner2026placementpreprint]. Our separate and common-sample analyses likewise show directional agreement but differences in magnitude or support. Chest adherence retained the main daytime and pre-sleep directions and interval conclusions, but provides no sleep ocular claim. We neither pooled positions nor inferred equivalence or a universal correction.

<!-- P-D02 -->
The consensus recommendations provide the clearest health-relevant benchmark. Only 24.0% of waking minutes reached the daytime range, while 63.3% of pre-sleep and 87.7% of bedside sleep-environment minutes met their upper limits. Modelled adherence was lower on free days during daytime and sleep but higher during pre-sleep, with site variation in daytime and sleep. Outdoor exposure exceeded the daytime recommendation, but all other conditions remained below it on average. The fitted sleep-category mean exceeded the bedside limit, while 87.7% of pooled bedside sleep-environment minutes met it. These quantities describe different aspects of the exposure distribution and should be interpreted together. These pooled fractions, recommendation-window proportions and hourly means are distinct estimands. Together, they identify intervention opportunities and shortfalls. The recommendations principally address healthy adults aged 18 to 55 with regular daytime schedules [@brown2022].

<!-- P-M12A -->
The main adherence analysis retained one row per window period and defined adherence as adherent valid minutes divided by all valid minutes in that period. A behavioural cycle crossed midnight when necessary and inherited the work or free label of the calendar date on which daytime began. The any-valid primary sample retained a window with at least one valid comparison; an otherwise identical coverage sensitivity required at least 80% coverage of the exact window interval. Exact 0% and 100% periods remained in an endpoint-inflated beta-binomial model. Fixed predictors represented the complete window-by-site-by-day-type pattern, and the retained random effect was a participant intercept in the beta-binomial mean. Population averages weighted all nine sites equally. Separate FDR families covered window-specific free-minus-work contrasts, interaction tests, site-specific contrasts against zero and 27 comparisons of site-specific contrasts with their window-specific equal-site values. Chest models were fitted separately for daytime and pre-sleep.

<!-- P-M12B -->
The exploratory cross-window extension used daytime adherence as the predictor and sleep or pre-sleep adherence as outcomes in daytime-anchored behavioural cycles. It separated each participant's cycle-specific daytime deviation from their observed average daytime adherence, while adjusting for window, site, day type, their corresponding interactions and the participant's mix of daytime observations across day types. The four within-participant and between-participant associations formed one FDR family. Positive serial dependence remained unresolved and some unchanged sensitivity fits were non-estimable, so no within-participant day-level claim was stated. Descriptive profiles used site-average participant means for 139 complete three-window profiles and 417 participant-window points; they neither identify nor rank participants and do not define stable participant types.
