# Evidence-supported positioning

## 1. What the bounded evidence directly establishes

Near-eye and eye-directed personal light measurement is not new by itself. Verified field studies extend from small 24-hour eye-level recordings in the 1980s to multi-day corneal-plane alpha-opic studies and week-long spectacle-mounted photopic studies. Some single-country spectacle-mounted studies analysed more participants than the target study's primary near-eye sample.

International personal-light comparison is also not new by itself. Verified studies have compared cohorts in two countries, and large wrist-based cohorts have covered multiple regions or tens of thousands of participants.

Within the bounded verified set, no independent comparator combined the target study's nine-site, seven-country scope with a shared repeated near-eye protocol, melanopic measurement, common contextual data, and a joint multiscale analysis.

## 2. What the methods evidence supports

The methods literature supports treating harmonization as scientifically consequential. Wearable light studies differ in device capability, placement, temporal handling, context ascertainment, wear and non-wear procedures, completeness rules, and metric definitions. A shared protocol and common analytical chain therefore improve interpretability across sites.

The methods and wearability literature also supports the claim that near-eye measurement is burdensome. Glasses-mounted devices require fitting and participant instruction, can be uncomfortable or conspicuous, and are sensitive to removal, occlusion, activity, charging, and environmental conditions. These constraints make a large, context-rich international near-eye dataset a substantial data-collection achievement. They do not guarantee freedom from measurement error.

## 3. What remains uncertain

This search cannot establish absolute priority. Several major subscription indexes and non-English sources were not searched, large result sets were rank-capped, and citation chaining was best effort. The absence of an exact comparator is therefore insufficient for the words *first* or *largest*.

The target study's site-specific recruitment history is not established by the external literature. The author has confirmed that willingness to wear the glasses constrained recruitment at some sites, especially Costa Rica, and that the chest option broadened recruitment possibilities. This implementation fact should be attributed to the study record or author-confirmed Methods documentation, not inferred from accepted sample counts alone.

## 4. Exact gap addressed by the study

The study provides a harmonized, repeated, near-eye account of everyday ocular light exposure across nine sites in seven countries, then organizes that exposure across temporal, geographic, behavioural, daily, participant, and measurement scales.

That gap is more precise than a generic claim of scale:

> Prior field studies have measured personal light near the eye, across countries, or in large cohorts, but these strengths have rarely been combined. Here, a shared protocol across nine sites in seven countries links repeated near-eye melanopic measurements with common temporal, environmental, behavioural, and participant context.

Use *rarely been combined* only if supported by the bounded-search citation set and paired with a transparent Methods or Discussion qualifier. A still safer formulation is:

> We used a shared protocol across nine sites in seven countries to characterize repeated near-eye ocular light exposure together with common temporal, environmental, behavioural, and participant context.

## 5. Why the gap matters

Public-health interpretation requires an everyday exposure baseline before interventions, policies, or health associations can be placed in context. The Brown et al. recommendations provide a physiologically grounded benchmark for healthy-light environments. The target study can show how measured minutes were distributed relative to those contextual ranges and which temporal and everyday conditions structured exposure.

The benchmark connection should be prominent but bounded:

- describe the recommendations as expert-consensus melanopic EDI targets supported by circadian, neuroendocrine, and alerting-response evidence;
- report fractions of valid measured minutes within the daytime, pre-sleep, and sleep-environment ranges;
- distinguish waking near-eye exposure from the bedside sleep environment;
- do not label people adherent or non-adherent;
- do not infer a health outcome, benefit, harm, or biological response from threshold position alone.

## Recommended title and claim language

### Preferred working title

**The international architecture of personal light exposure**

This title is defensible because:

- *international* is directly supported by nine sites in seven countries;
- *architecture* is a conceptual claim about the organization of exposure across time and nested contextual scales, not a claim of causal determination or priority; and
- *personal light exposure* is concise title language, while the Introduction should define the construct immediately as ocular light exposure measured near the eye.

To keep *architecture* concrete, the title and opening summary must be followed by a definition such as:

> Here, architecture refers to the recurring daily pattern of ocular light exposure and the nested variation associated with site, season, daily context, day, and participant.

### Fallback working title

**Personal and daily context outweighs geography in an international study of light exposure**

This is empirically sharper but depends more strongly on model-specific fitted-curve and allocation summaries. It should remain a fallback until the full abstract is balanced against all accepted qualifications.

### Preferred distinctiveness wording

- “a harmonized near-eye study across nine sites in seven countries”
- “an unusually broad harmonized international study of near-eye ocular light exposure”
- “a shared international protocol linking near-eye melanopic exposure with repeated everyday context”

### Wording to avoid

- “the first international study”
- “the largest near-eye study”
- “global cohort”
- “recommendation adherence” or “compliance”
- “healthy” or “unhealthy” exposure profiles
- “determinants” when a causal reading is possible
- rhetorical or deterministic language that is not scientifically defined

## Public related placement evidence

The public bioRxiv version 2 preprint by Zauner et al., DOI `10.64898/2026.07.28.741277`, should be cited when the Nature Health manuscript explains that measurement-position differences are structured rather than reducible to a single offset. The preprint reports that the direction and magnitude of differences depend on analytical scale, metric class, context, site, day, and participant, with timing outcomes generally less placement-sensitive than level and temporal-dynamics outcomes. It does not support pooling positions or applying a universal correction.

The placement preprint and the Nature Health manuscript use overlapping MeLiDos data. It is therefore related public evidence, not independent replication. The Nature Health paper should cite it for the measurement principle, retain its own accepted paired and placement-specific results as the direct evidence for this manuscript, and disclose the overlap in the cover letter and related-work statement.

## Strongest defensible contribution

The strongest contribution is not a single exposure association or a priority claim. It is a multiscale, international baseline of everyday ocular light exposure that connects physiologically relevant recommendation contexts with the temporal, environmental, behavioural, daily, participant, and measurement conditions under which exposure occurs.
