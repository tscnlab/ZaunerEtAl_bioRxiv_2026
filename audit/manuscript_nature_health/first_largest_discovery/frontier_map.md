# Evidence frontier: near-eye, international, and harmonized personal light exposure

## Decision in brief

The verified literature supports the working title **The international architecture of personal light exposure** as a conceptual description of the study. The title does not assert priority, and the accepted study record substantiates the international scope.

The bounded evidence does **not** support an unqualified claim that the study is the first or largest near-eye personal-light study. Spectacle-mounted field studies with 179 and 212 analysed participants exceed the target primary near-eye sample of 141 participants, and eye-level or corneal-plane field measurement predates this study.

No independently verified comparator in the bounded set combined all of the following:

- a shared protocol across nine sites in seven countries;
- repeated free-living measurement near the eye;
- physiologically weighted melanopic quantities;
- common contextual information on environment and behaviour; and
- a joint multiscale analysis of temporal, site, day, participant, and immediate-context variation.

That observed gap justifies a precise distinctiveness statement, not a priority claim.

## Measurement, calibration, and reporting methods

- Guidolin et al. 2024 verifies that the target programme was designed around a shared prospective multicentre protocol with near-corneal, chest, wrist, and contextual measurements.
- van Duijnhoven et al. 2025 documents a wide wearable-device landscape and substantial variation in capabilities and placement.
- Hartmeyer et al. 2023 and Hartmeyer and Andersen 2024 show why measurement, preparation, and metric choices need explicit harmonization for cross-study comparability.
- Spitschan et al. 2022 separates technical verification, analytical validation, clinical validation, and usability. Physiological proximity does not by itself establish clinical validity.
- Stampfli et al. 2023 documents the device chain for near-corneal alpha-opic measurement.
- Zauner et al. 2026 shows why wear, occlusion, auxiliary data, time handling, and participant briefing are integral to quality control rather than incidental logistics.
- de Vries et al. 2026 provides further methods evidence that wear position and in-situ factors change dosimeter performance.
- Zauner et al. 2026 bioRxiv v2 directly evaluates simultaneous glasses, chest, and wrist records from the overlapping MeLiDos cohort. It establishes outcome- and context-dependent placement differences and supports prospective, metric-specific placement choices rather than a universal correction.

## Temporal, state, gap, and completeness frontier

The literature consistently treats temporal coverage and wear state as consequential, but implementation remains heterogeneous. The target study's accepted preparation record is therefore part of the contribution:

- repeated exposure is represented at minute, half-hour, hour, day, and participant scales;
- sleep-period sensor values are interpreted as the bedside sleep environment;
- near-eye non-wear and occlusion require explicit handling;
- unsupported intervals and metrics cannot be silently converted into exposure; and
- chest and near-eye data remain separate, with paired common-sample comparisons where appropriate.

This search did not attempt to re-audit those accepted rules. It establishes only that transparent temporal and state handling is necessary for comparison with prior work.

## Health-relevant benchmark frontier

Brown et al. 2022 provides expert-consensus melanopic EDI recommendations grounded in human circadian, neuroendocrine, and alerting-response evidence:

- at least 250 lx during daytime at the eye;
- at most 10 lx beginning at least three hours before bed; and
- at most 1 lx in the sleep environment, which should be as dark as possible.

The recommendations principally concern healthy adults aged 18 to 55 with regular daytime schedules. They are not individual risk thresholds, and the paper notes that quantifying direct benefits of implementation remains challenging.

For the target study, the valid connection is a descriptive comparison of measured minutes with these contextual ranges. The comparison can establish an everyday exposure baseline and show how often observations fell within the recommended ranges. It cannot classify people as adherent, estimate a health effect, or infer biological response.

## Recruitment and measurement-position frontier

The author has clarified that some sites, especially Costa Rica, had difficulty recruiting people willing to wear the near-eye glasses. The chest option therefore had a recruitment-feasibility role in addition to its analytical role.

Three propositions must remain distinct:

1. **Accepted numerical coverage:** the accepted Descriptives report contains separate near-eye, chest, and paired sample counts.
2. **Accepted analytical interpretation:** paired common-sample evidence relates the two placements, but they are not pooled and no universal correction is inferred.
3. **Author-confirmed implementation fact:** the chest option broadened recruitment possibilities where willingness to wear the glasses was limited, especially in Costa Rica.
4. **Public related analysis:** the parallel bioRxiv v2 preprint shows structured position differences across contexts and outcomes using an overlapping cohort. It can be cited for the measurement principle, but it is not independent confirmation of this manuscript's accepted results.

External wearability studies support the plausibility of this recruitment challenge. They do not independently document the target study's site-specific recruitment history.

## Unresolved and contradictory evidence

- No verified record in the bounded set matches the full combined design, but this is an observed search result rather than proof of first occurrence.
- The title and abstract language of some source papers use broad novelty wording. Those author claims were not treated as independent verification.
- The target programme's Ghana report overlaps the target cohort and was excluded as an independent comparator.
- The public placement preprint also overlaps the target cohort. It is included because the manuscript should cite the related analysis, but it is not counted as independent replication or novelty evidence.
- One Zenodo identifier linked to the target study was retained as unverified because its authoritative metadata were not inspected. It does not affect the positioning decision.

## Gaps in the bounded search

- Subscription citation indexes, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, ProQuest, and discipline-specific engineering databases were not searched.
- The largest Europe PMC query sets were screened only through the first 100 service-ranked records.
- Non-English literature was outside scope.
- Conference proceedings and older device reports may be under-indexed.
- No full systematic-review protocol or dual independent screening was used.

These gaps are why the manuscript should use **a harmonized near-eye study across nine sites in seven countries** or **an unusually broad harmonized international study of ocular light exposure**, not **the first** or **the largest**.
