34 Abstract

35 Personal light exposure has been associated with sleep and non-communicable diseases,
36 yet how ocular exposure varies across the day, among people and between everyday
37 settings remains poorly understood. We analysed 191 adults at nine sites in seven countries;
38 184 contributed 1,478 participant-days (near eye: 141 adults, 816 days; complementary
39 chest: 154 adults, 902 days). The shared 24-hour near-eye pattern accounted for 78.9% of
40 full-model R², participant patterns 12.9%, participant-day shifts 6.2% and site patterns 2.0%.
41 Reported light source and immediate setting produced large contrasts, accounting for 24.2%
42 and 30.7%, respectively, of variation in fitted hourly patterns, the next-largest shares after
43 time of day. Associations with site, day length and personal characteristics differed across
44 exposure measures. Only 24.0% of daytime minutes met the recommendation. By resolving
45 variation across sites, people, days and immediate settings, this study lays an empirical
46 foundation for targeted, harmonised exposure monitoring aligned with health-based light
47 recommendations.
