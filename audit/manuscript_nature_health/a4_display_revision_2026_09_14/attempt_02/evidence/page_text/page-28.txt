436 Exploratory inverse associations of average daytime with sleep and pre-sleep adherence
437 show why the three recommendation windows should not be collapsed into one score. They
438 may reflect schedules, environments or other between-participant differences, not a within-
439 person trade-off or stable phenotype. In exploratory nonlinear models, light source and
440 immediate setting accounted for substantially more variation in fitted hourly patterns than
441 participant-specific patterns: 24.2% versus 7.6%, and 30.7% versus 8.4%, respectively. This
442 finding is consistent with schedules and immediate environments being prominent correlates
443 of hourly exposure, but it is not a causal explanation for the cross-window adherence
444 association. Longitudinal models of dependence and changing routines are needed to test
445 whether improving one part of the pattern displaces or supports another.

446 Location mattered, but not as one north-to-south ranking. Site differences concentrated in
447 daily level, waking bright-light duration and several timing metrics, while dose, spectrum and
448 regularity did not show the same site pattern. Civil photoperiod was associated with a
449 broader set of metrics, with larger day-length associations for bright-light level and dose than
450 for the darkest daily window. Between 14.2 and 16.2 h of civil photoperiod, six of nine
451 metrics showed signs of uncertainty about a photoperiod effect beyond that point. Sites were
452 sampled in distinct calendar periods and covered unequal photoperiod ranges, leaving
453 latitude, season, weather, temperature, culture, mobility and the built environment entangled.
454 Repeated seasonal sampling across more sites is needed to separate these influences.

455 Immediate setting patterned exposure, consistent with environmental opportunity, built
456 settings and behaviour36. Outdoor daylight and being outdoors exceeded indoor electric light
457 and awake time at home, respectively; active days exceeded sedentary days. Site
458 interactions placed outdoors-category exposure higher in Borås (SE) than Tübingen (DE),
459 while the free-versus-work association was most evident in Borås (SE) and Dortmund (DE).
460 Participant intercepts captured remaining between-participant variation, not individual
461 responses or predictions. Future monitoring should preserve local clock time, location,
462 immediate setting, daylight access and site; self-selection, occupation, mobility and weather
463 remain potential confounders.
