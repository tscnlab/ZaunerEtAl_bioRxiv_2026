265 Immediate environments and routines reorganise exposure

266 Self-reported light source was strongly associated with one-hour near-eye exposure, with an
267 FDR-supported light-source-by-site interaction. Site-average interaction-model estimates
268 were 959.6 lx melanopic EDI for outdoor daylight (95% CI 786.8 to 1,170.2), 198.8 lx for
269 indoor daylight (169.3 to 233.4), 85.1 lx for indoor electric light (71.6 to 101.1) and 23.8 lx for
270 a display screen (17.4 to 32.5). Outdoor daylight was 11.27 times the indoor-electric
271 estimate (8.71 to 14.60) and exceeded the 250-lx daytime recommendation, whereas the
272 indoor-daylight and indoor-electric intervals were below it. During reported sleep, darkness
273 and external-light estimates were 2.24 lx (1.54 to 3.26) and 11.18 lx (8.06 to 15.51), both
274 above the 1-lx bedside upper limit. Outdoor electric light remained imprecise relative to
275 indoor electric light near eye (ratio 1.019, 0.336 to 3.090; FDR-adjusted p = 0.973). These
276 contextual averages do not classify people or hours as adherent.

277 External light during sleep in Munich (DE) was estimated at 130.2 lx, 11.65 times the
278 corresponding site average; sleep darkness in Kumasi (GH) was 0.371 lx, 0.166 times its
279 site average. Sparse site-category cells limited some estimates.

280 The primary analysis used a population-mean quasi-Tweedie model with participant-cluster-
281 robust covariance for uncertainty; lag-one residual correlation remained 0.288. Fitted versus
282 observed exact-zero proportions were 39.8% and 27.8%, respectively. An exploratory
283 mixed-effects model quantified variance explained after adding a participant intercept to site-
284 by-light-source fixed terms. Marginal R² was 0.796 and conditional R² 0.876, an 8.0-
285 percentage-point increase in explained variance from the participant intercept. Of the
286 marginal R², light source accounted for 89.3%, site 7.1% and their interaction 3.7%.
287 Separately, an exploratory time-of-day model attributed 60.4% of variation in fitted hourly
288 patterns to global time of day and 24.2% to light-source deviations. Among these two
289 components, light source accounted for 28.6%. The remaining shares were 5.2% for site,
290 7.6% for participant patterns and 2.5% for day-to-day shifts.

291 A sensitivity adjusted the population-average light-source comparisons for each participant’s
292 overall category distribution, distinguishing hour-to-hour differences within participants from
293 differences in category composition between participants. The overall category pattern was
294 retained. For external light during sleep, the near-eye ratio relative to indoor electric light
295 changed from 0.449 in the primary additive model to 0.333 (95% CI 0.190 to 0.585), a 25.8%
296 decrease. Thus, between-participant category composition influenced the magnitude, but not
297 the direction, of this contrast. Supplementary Figure S8 shows the temporal, support and
298 site-specific results.
