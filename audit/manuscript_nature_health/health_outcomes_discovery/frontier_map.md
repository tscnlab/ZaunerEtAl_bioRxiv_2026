# Health-outcome evidence frontier

## Direct population evidence

- Sleep timing and sleepiness: Didikoglu et al. measured wrist melanopic EDI for seven days in 59 UK adults. This is the closest verified record for a physiologically weighted quantity, but remains wrist based and single-country.
- Psychiatric outcomes: Burns et al. analysed objective wrist-light profiles in more than 85,000 UK Biobank participants. The study is large but cross-sectional for psychiatric outcomes.
- Metabolic outcomes: Windred et al. prospectively related wrist-light profiles to incident type 2 diabetes in 84,790 participants. Obayashi et al. supplied smaller longitudinal bedside-light evidence for obesity change, while Kim et al. supplied cross-sectional wrist-light evidence for obesity, diabetes and hypertension in older adults.
- Cardiovascular outcomes: Windred et al. prospectively related brighter night light to five incident cardiovascular outcomes in 88,905 participants.
- Mortality: Windred et al. prospectively related brighter nights and darker days to all-cause and cardiometabolic mortality in 88,905 participants.

## Exposure-baseline evidence

Wallace et al. characterised level, duration, timing and regularity in 4,067 adults from two US cohorts across eight regions. Its broad 24-hour rhythm and geographic patterning show that a daily rhythm itself is established. Its wrist placement, photopic quantity, US-only design and cohort-specific analysis leave the international near-eye amplitude and nested decomposition unresolved.

## Physiology-based benchmark

Brown et al. provides expert recommendations for ocular melanopic EDI during daytime and before sleep, and for the sleep environment during sleep. These are contextual recommendations derived from physiological response evidence. They are not participant-level adherence categories, individual risk functions or causal thresholds for the present observational data.

## Main unresolved bridge

The bounded evidence connects personal light to several health-relevant outcomes, but largely through wrist or bedside broad-spectrum measurements and one week of exposure. It does not establish how a harmonised near-eye melanopic pattern is distributed across international sites, how its amplitude differs by local clock time, or how common time, site, participant, day and immediate context contribute to that distribution.

## Gaps and contradictions

- Direct retinal dose is absent. Near-eye measurement is closer to the incident ocular field but remains a proxy.
- Large cohorts are concentrated in UK Biobank and therefore share population, device and exposure-processing features.
- Health analyses and the Nature Health exposure-baseline study target different estimands. Their numerical thresholds and ratios are not interchangeable.
- Long-term exposure stability is incompletely resolved because the largest health cohorts used approximately one week of light measurement.
- Causal effects and intervention benefits remain outside the bounded observational evidence.

The absence of an exact international near-eye match in this bounded set does not establish a first or largest claim.
