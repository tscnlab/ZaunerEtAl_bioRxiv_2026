# Nature Health targeting memo

Date checked: 2026-08-13

Proposed content type: **Article**

This memo separates explicit journal instructions from manuscript-worker inferences about editorial fit. It is not a submission decision and does not contact the journal.

## Recommendation

Develop the paper as a Nature Health Article, but treat journal fit as conditional rather than established. The study is plainly within the journal's named environmental-health and observational remit. Its strongest fit is as international exposure epidemiology that establishes an everyday ocular-light baseline relative to physiology-based recommendations and identifies how exposure varies across geographic, temporal, behavioural, daily, participant, and measurement scales. Its two serious desk-rejection risks are also plain: no health outcome was measured, and the current project record does not document the meaningful participant and community engagement that the journal says it requires.

The manuscript should therefore make a strong exposure-science contribution without implying a measured health effect. Before submission staging, the authors should resolve whether factual engagement evidence exists and decide whether the exposure-only contribution is strong enough for Nature Health's health-policy and practice criterion.

The following items are stated on official Nature Health or Nature Portfolio pages and were accessed on 2026-08-13.

### Scope and editorial criteria

- Nature Health evaluates research for originality, timeliness, and impact on health policy and practice.
- It publishes health research across medical, social, and environmental sciences.

### Content type and Article format

Nature Health lists Article, Brief Communication, Resource, Analysis, and several non-primary formats. For an Article it states:

- main text up to 4,000 words, excluding abstract, Methods, references, and figure legends;
- unreferenced abstract up to 150 words;
- up to six combined main display items, figures and tables;
- an unheaded Introduction followed by Results, Discussion, and Methods;
- topical subheadings in Results and Methods, with no subheadings in Discussion;
- typically up to about 60 references as a guideline;
- optional Supplementary Information.

Source: [Nature Health Content Types](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/content), accessed 2026-08-13.

### Audience and language

Submissions should be accessible to non-specialists. Basic scientific knowledge can be assumed, but field-specific concepts should be explained. Technical jargon should be avoided or defined, abbreviations kept to a minimum, the rationale and main conclusions made explicit, and the title and abstract understandable to any scientist.

Sources: [Writing and language](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/submission-guidelines/writing-and-language) and [AIP and formatting](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/submission-guidelines/aip-and-formatting), accessed 2026-08-13.

### Initial submission and supporting material

- Initial submission requires a manuscript file and cover letter. Supplementary Information is optional.
- The manuscript must give enough detail for a fellow expert to replicate the study.
- The cover letter should explain importance and fit for the diverse readership, disclose related manuscripts, and state prior discussions with a Nature Health editor.
- Up to ten Extended Data display items are permitted on the preparation page.
- Initial submissions may be PDF, Word, or TeX or LaTeX without special formatting.

Sources: [Preparing your material](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/submission-guidelines/preparing-your-submission) and [Initial formatting](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/submission-guidelines/initial-formatting), accessed 2026-08-13.

### Final format, figures, tables, and Supplementary Information

- Final source must be Word or TeX or LaTeX, not PDF.
- Tables are placed at the end of the text document. Statistical table legends identify error analysis and ranges.
- Main figures are cited in sequence, use editable labelling, are at least 300 dpi for bitmap panels, and are no wider than 180 mm.
- Figure legends provide a brief title, panel descriptions, centre and error definitions, sample size, statistical test, and p values where applicable.
- Statistical source data should be supplied in Excel, one file per relevant figure.
- Supplementary text, figures, simple tables, and legends are combined in one PDF, cited and numbered separately, and each Supplementary Figure plus legend fits one page.

Source: [AIP and formatting](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/submission-guidelines/aip-and-formatting), accessed 2026-08-13.

These are final-format requirements. They should guide the Quarto architecture now, but they are not all initial-submission obligations.

### Reporting, data, code, preregistration, and ethics

- A Nature Portfolio Reporting Summary is required for relevant life, behavioural and social, and environmental research sent for review and is published with accepted manuscripts.
- All original research includes a Data Availability statement describing access to the minimum dataset and any restrictions.
- Custom code central to the conclusions is available to editors and reviewers on request and is described in a separate Code availability section after Data Availability and before references.
- Preregistration details should be provided with submission.
- Observational cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional studies must be reported according to STROBE.
- Human-participant research states Declaration of Helsinki compliance, identifies the approving ethics committee and reference number, and states that informed consent was obtained.
- Sex and gender are distinguished, their determination and categorization described, and relevant analyses reported or justified.
- Substantive large-language-model use is documented in Methods, and LLMs cannot be authors.

Sources: [Reporting standards and availability](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/editorial-policies/reporting-standards), [Clinical Research](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/editorial-policies/clinical-research), [Research Ethics](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/editorial-policies/ethics-and-biosecurity), [Artificial Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/editorial-policies/ai), and [Preparing your material](https://www.nature.com/naturehealth/submission-guidelines/preparing-your-submission), accessed 2026-08-13.

## Manuscript-worker inference about fit

The following judgments are inferences, not explicit promises or rules from the journal.

### Why the paper could fit

1. **Named scope match.** The study is observational environmental-health research with an international population perspective.
2. **Methodological advance.** Harmonized near-eye measurement at nine sites provides a more direct representation of ocular light exposure than common wrist or chest sensing, while the paired placement evidence defines what can and cannot be transferred across positions.
3. **Health-relevant exposure baseline.** The accepted Brown-context table shows how valid waking, pre-sleep, and sleep-environment minutes were distributed relative to melanopic recommendations derived from human circadian, neuroendocrine, and alerting-response evidence.
4. **Population-exposure contribution.** The study quantifies the relative importance of geographic context, within-site personal and daily variation, immediate environment, routines, and selected individual characteristics.
5. **Real-world relevance.** The data identify contexts and time scales that matter for exposure assessment, surveillance, future cohort design, and eventual linkage to health outcomes.
6. **Difficult measurement at useful scale.** The harmonized dataset required active fitting, instruction, contextual reporting, and wear-quality handling for a near-eye sensor that can be removed or occluded and that creates participant burden.
7. **Broad readership.** The story can speak to environmental health, public health, epidemiology, sleep and circadian science, wearable sensing, building and lighting research, and behavioural science if specialist language is controlled.

### Why editorial fit remains uncertain

1. **No health endpoint.** The study does not test disease, symptoms, physiology, wellbeing, or intervention response. The manuscript can establish exposure architecture, not health impact.
2. **Engagement requirement.** Recruitment, consent, participation, and collaboration among study sites are not automatically meaningful engagement with participants and communities. The current record does not establish compliance with the journal's stated requirement.
3. **Policy or practice impact must be argued carefully.** A measurement baseline can inform surveillance and study design, but the present evidence does not show that a particular policy or intervention improves health.
4. **Site sample is not population-representative.** The international scope is wide, but the participants and collection periods do not estimate national prevalence or global exposure distributions.
5. **Large analytical corpus versus tight Article format.** A six-item and 4,000-word limit requires a disciplined hierarchy. Complete reporting must move to Extended Data or Supplementary Information without hiding nulls or qualifications.

## Article type decision

**Recommended: Article.** The study reports original, multi-part research with a central conceptual claim and needs topical Results and Methods sections. A Brief Communication is too short. An Analysis is described mainly as a new analysis of existing large datasets or meta-analyses and is a weaker match because this project generated the measurements. A Resource is plausible only if the authors want the reusable dataset itself, rather than the scientific explanation of exposure architecture, to be the primary contribution. The recommended narrative is therefore an Article, with the dataset's utility presented as a major strength rather than the sole article type rationale.

## Implications for framing

- Define the construct at first use as **ocular light exposure measured near the eye**. Use **personal light exposure** as the concise recurring term where the ocular meaning is unambiguous. Introduce **personal light exposome** only if carefully defined and used as a framing term, not as a claim that the full social and environmental exposome was measured.
- Replace **determinants** with **architecture**, **correlates**, **variation**, or **context**.
- Lead with the multiscale result: shared daily structure, substantial within-site person and day variation, strong proximal context, partial geographic modulation, and selective person-level associations.
- Treat outdoor daylight and outdoor activity contrasts as quantitative anchors, not surprising discoveries.
- State once in the abstract and directly in the Discussion that no health outcome was measured.
- Express implications as exposure assessment, measurement strategy, surveillance, cohort design, and future health-linkage priorities.
- Do not claim prevention, clinical translation, personalized intervention, or policy benefit from the current observational associations.
- Keep near-eye primary and chest complementary throughout. Do not use chest as a replication label unless the specific accepted result supports that language.
- Give the Brown recommendation-context comparison a prominent main-Results role. Treat it as contextual fractions of valid measured minutes, not adherence, adequacy, individual risk, or measured biological response.
- Use the author-preferred working title **The international architecture of personal light exposure**. The bounded search supports it as a conceptual title but not an unqualified first or largest claim.
- Separate the chest channel's paired analytical role from its recruitment-feasibility role at sites where willingness to wear the light glasses was limited.
- Cite the public bioRxiv version 2 placement preprint, DOI `10.64898/2026.07.28.741277`, for structured measurement-position differences. State that it uses overlapping MeLiDos data and is related evidence rather than independent replication.

## Implications for manuscript architecture

The Article limit argues for five topical Results sections plus one short measurement and robustness section:

1. international ocular-exposure baseline, Brown recommendation contexts, and daily architecture;
2. personal and day-to-day variation relative to sites;
3. geography and photoperiod as partial context;
4. immediate environment and daily routines;
5. selective person-level correlates and informative null findings;
6. placement, recruitment feasibility, and robustness boundaries.

The complete hypothesis mapping, metric guide, model detail, category results, null matrices, diagnostics, sensitivities, placement comparisons, and preregistration deviations should remain traceable in Methods and Supplementary Information.
