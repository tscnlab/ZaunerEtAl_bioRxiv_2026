## Ocular-exposure terminology

The measured construct is ocular light exposure. The manuscript does not need to repeat the ocular qualifier in every sentence, but it must define the construct clearly at first use and avoid vague light wording where placement or physiological meaning matters.

## Brown recommendation connection

Assessment against the Brown et al. recommendations is an important health-relevant connection point even though it is not the analytical centre of the manuscript.

The narrative should integrate:

- the accepted descriptive recommendation-context table;
- the accepted time-varying and temporal analyses that help explain when exposure falls within or outside the contexts;
- specific exposure metrics whose definitions and rationale relate directly to the recommended ranges; and
- explicit separation of a physiologically grounded exposure benchmark from measured health outcomes.

Protected interpretation:

- report fractions of valid measured minutes in the recommended contextual ranges;
- do not classify participants as adherent or non-adherent;
- do not infer health benefit, harm, or biological response from threshold position alone;
- preserve the bedside sleep-environment interpretation.

## Measurement-position implications

Near-eye measurement remains primary for ocular exposure. Chest measurement remains separate complementary evidence. No simple pooling, equivalence claim, universal correction, or conversion is authorized.

The two implications are distinct:

1. paired common-observation analyses relate near-eye and chest results while preserving separate estimands; and
2. the chest option broadened recruitment possibilities because some sites had difficulty finding people willing to wear the light glasses, especially Costa Rica.

The second point concerns willingness and recruitment feasibility, not analytical eligibility. It is an author-confirmed implementation fact and should be documented as such in the Methods and limitations.

## Public related placement analysis

The author identified the public bioRxiv version 2 preprint **Sensor placement causes outcome-dependent bias in ambulatory light-exposure estimates**, DOI `10.64898/2026.07.28.741277`, as the parallel wearing-position analysis that should be cited for structured differences among measurement positions.

The exact v2 identity and full text were verified on 2026-08-13. The Nature Health manuscript should use it to support the qualitative measurement principle that position differences vary by analytical scale, metric class, context, site, day, and participant and therefore cannot be represented by a universal correction.

Protected interpretation:

- the preprint uses overlapping MeLiDos data and is related evidence, not independent replication;
- the Nature Health manuscript's accepted paired and placement-specific analyses remain the direct evidence for its own claims;
- the citation does not authorize importing unaccepted estimates into this manuscript;
- the overlap and parallel submission should be disclosed in the related-work statement and cover letter; and
- the verified public preprint supersedes the earlier cover-letter-only citation hold.

## Strengths emphasis

The Discussion should recognize the effort required to collect a large international near-eye dataset with rich contextual information. Near-eye sensing is not a passive sensor that can simply be attached and ignored. It requires researcher fitting and participant instruction, creates wear burden, and is sensitive to removal, occlusion, activity, and other real-world conditions.

This effort is a study strength when paired with the explicit quality and state-handling procedures. Burden itself is not evidence of validity.
