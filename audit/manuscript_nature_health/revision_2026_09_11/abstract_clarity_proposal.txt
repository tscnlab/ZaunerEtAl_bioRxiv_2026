Personal light exposure has been associated with sleep and non-communicable diseases, yet how ocular exposure varies in everyday life remains poorly understood. We studied 191 adults at nine sites in seven countries; 184 contributed 1,478 participant-days (near eye: 141 adults, 816 days; complementary chest: 154 adults, 902 days). A shared 24-hour near-eye pattern accounted for 78.9% of the variation explained by the daily-pattern model. Differences among people and days outweighed differences between sites. Light source and immediate setting showed large hourly contrasts, and differences between work and free days varied by site. Associations with site, day length and personal characteristics differed across exposure measures. Only 24.0% of daytime minutes met the health-based light recommendation. By locating variation across sites, people, days and settings, this study provides an empirical foundation for targeted, harmonised exposure monitoring aligned with health-based light recommendations.
