# Title and abstract proposals for author decision

These are proposals only. The manuscript title and abstract text have not been changed.

## PI title

The health-relevant architecture of the everyday light exposome

The title connects the multiscale findings with environmental-health research and the health-based recommendations. "Everyday" matches the field measurements, and "architecture" accommodates timing, amplitude, setting and variation among sites, people and days. "Health-relevant" is supportable through the established physiology, external population literature and recommendation comparison, not through measured health outcomes in this study.

The main risk is breadth. The exposome framework concerns exposures across the life course; this study records a limited everyday component, not a complete lifetime exposome. "Light" also needs early specification as near-eye ocular exposure during wear, with complementary non-ocular chest measurements and bedside interpretation during sleep. Replacing "multiscale" loses a useful description of the study's specific contribution. The proposed title is defensible if these boundaries remain explicit.

| Location | Proposed adjustment |
|---|---|
| P-I02 | Connect the established exposome definition to this study's time-resolved ocular-light component, without implying lifetime or global coverage. |
| P-I04 | Replace the final definitional sentence with the operational definition below. Retain the explicit non-global scope and prospect of harmonised measurement at further sites. |
| P-D01 and P-D09 | Echo the architecture of the daily light component of the exposome and its value for exposure monitoring and recommendation-based benchmarking. Do not recast associations as causal health findings. |
| P-D08 | Retain the explicit statement that no health outcome was measured. |

Suggested P-I04 definition:

> We use architecture to describe how this daily component of the exposome is organised across time, sites, people, days and immediate settings. Its health relevance is assessed against established light recommendations, not health outcomes measured in this study.

A narrower alternative, if preferred, is *The health-relevant architecture of everyday ocular light exposure*.

## Less technical abstract

134 words under a whitespace-separated structural count. Brown-related content is subject to the pending accepted replacement where applicable. This proposal retains the established descriptive daytime fraction; it introduces no new estimate.

> Personal light exposure has been associated with sleep and non-communicable diseases, yet its everyday distribution remains poorly understood. We studied 191 adults at nine sites in seven countries; 184 contributed 1,478 participant-days (near eye: 141 adults, 816 days; complementary chest: 154 adults, 902 days). A shared 24-hour rhythm accounted for 78.9% of the variation explained by the daily-pattern model. Differences among people and days outweighed differences between sites. Light source and immediate setting showed large hourly contrasts, and differences between work and free days varied by site. Associations with site, day length and personal characteristics differed across exposure measures. Only 24.0% of daytime minutes met the health-based light recommendation. By locating variation across sites, people, days and settings, this study provides an empirical foundation for targeted, harmonised exposure monitoring aligned with health-based light recommendations.

The model phrase identifies the denominator and does not call the 78.9% share raw response variance. The new abstract is less technical because it omits the full component-percentage list and specialist decomposition terminology, while retaining the sample, central daily-pattern result, context and routine findings, metric-specific findings, and health-recommendation connection.
